# partsmith-features

`Feature` trait, `Registry`, `BuildContext`, `FeatureOutput`, and the built-in features (sketch, extrude, revolve, fillet, chamfer, shell, hole, primitives, booleans, patterns, mirror, datums, and more).

Spec (the definition of done): `docs/spec/features.md` and `docs/spec/features/<type>.md`. Acceptance tests in `tests/` come from features.md sections 6.2 and 6.3; document tests of section 6.4 and of every feature spec live in `crates/partsmith-recompute/tests/`. Do not weaken them.

## Public interface

The modules of features.md section 2.1 are public: `feature`, `deps`, `params`, `context`, `output`, `error` (errors, warnings and message parts), `registry` (with `register_builtins`), `python` and `builtin` (one module per type). The crate root re-exports everything below.

## Internals

- `deserializer.rs` reads typed params straight from the JSON map with its own serde error type, so `InvalidParams` details name the deepest field path without parsing serde messages, and its `field` is that path (or the missing top-level key), labeled from the manifest (section 3.2 step 2). Unknown root keys are the input keys the params schema does not know: `schema::known_keys` collects the root `properties` and those of every root `$ref`, `anyOf`, `oneOf` and `allOf` branch, so the fields of a flattened enum are known keys, reserved-name checked and need hints.
- `body_rules.rs` measures the body rules of `apply_op` (section 3.15): why nothing changed from the tight boxes along a world axis, the attach face, inner shells and `reverse_hits`. `error/body_rules.rs` renders their messages and fixes, and `error/too_large.rs` those of the "too large" errors with `Limit` (section 4.3).
- `schema.rs` holds the registration checks (open object root, reserved keys, `$defs` conflicts, manifest against schema through `$ref`, `anyOf`, `oneOf`, `allOf`, `items` and `prefixItems`).
- `profile.rs` builds the region union by dropping boundary pieces that two selected regions share (matched exactly, both directions) and chaining the rest through the arrangement's own loop successors; components (joined by shared pieces) give one outer loop each. `merge_pieces` then joins consecutive pieces of a loop (the last and the first included) that come from one entity, run the same way and meet exactly, into one line or arc, on every loop (a single region's too); a loop that is one entity all the way round becomes its circle, and otherwise a run never grows back to its own start (`closes`), so a boundary that touches itself on a circle keeps that circle's arcs apart. Pieces of an entity with another piece between them stay separate (naming numbers them `#n`).
- `context/` holds `BuildContext`, its reference lookups and `CancelToken` (`mod.rs`), planes, axes and face placements (`placements.rs`: `plane`, `axis`, `face_placement` with `FacePlacement` and `FaceFallback`, `face_placement_before_removal`, which passes a state without building its element set when its names hold no face of the query's family and no shell rim (`may_resolve_by_meaning`), the flat faces in one plane that the face placement rule reads, the hint in its frame, the side an axis keeps, and what their fields can use), `BodyState` with its cached measurements (`body_state.rs`), `Values` (`values.rs`) and, for tests only, the fixtures its unit tests share (`fixtures.rs`); `mod.rs` re-exports `BodyState`, `FaceFallback`, `FacePlacement` and `Values`, so `crate::context::BodyState` and the root exports are unchanged.
- `context/references.rs` resolves stored references by meaning (section 3.8: follows, guesses, widenings, losses, the candidates a field can use and the "one carrier" test; `picked_references` stores resolved elements as picks do, in name order): `EntryResolver` gives one outcome per entry, resolved or lost, and `resolve_all_usable` either merges them in entry order or, when no element resolved, makes `ReferencesNotFound` of their losses; `context/geometry.rs` holds the kernel sub-shapes and geometries of resolved elements (`sub_shapes`, `element_geometries`) and the geometry tests that the "one carrier" test and the plane and axis rules of section 3.6 share (`one_carrier`, `coplanar`, `same_line`, `same_axis`); `context/regions.rs` resolves profile regions (section 3.7).
- `error/` holds `FeatureError` (`mod.rs`), `FeatureWarning` (`warning.rs`), `FieldRef` and field paths (`field.rs`), fixes (`fix.rs`), losses and repairs (`loss.rs`, with the crate-private `Loss::fitting`, the ambiguous loss of the elements a `not_coplanar`, `not_collinear` or widening repair offers), the render model (`render.rs`: `RenderContext`, `Diagnosis` and the crate's `Renderer` with the shared reason and fix helpers of section 4.4), element descriptions (`describe/`: `mod.rs` the rules for edges and vertices (a vertex on one face reads by its position), `face.rs` face facts and words (a cap a cut records absent with `EndOutside` makes its element read as the tool's own, corpus R30 (a), and so does a tool face of a cut boolean of which the state has no piece, R30 (b)), `caps.rs` the direction a cap of an extrude or revolve faces in this run when it is not in the state (a revolve's from the axis in its record, the side of it the first region's stored centroid lies on and, for an end cap after a partial turn, `Names::value` of its angle; none for the end cap of a full turn), `cuts.rs` the cut boolean whose tool faces a feature's faces are in the body described (the faces followed through the later records that built in this run, up to the feature the state is just before: booleans, keeping their tools or not, and mirrors, patterns and splits into a target), `maker.rs` the feature that made a face; face names are read through partsmith-naming's `ElementName::face_name` view), and the message parts (`message.rs`: names, `format_value`, and the suggested-limit text, which cuts two significant digits from the shortest decimal form so it never exceeds the value).
- `test_support.rs` (tests only) has a `Fixture` that owns a context's inputs, `values` (evaluated uses), `reference` (a stored reference from its JSON), named box and primitive tools, `ScriptNames` for Python hooks, and helpers that build a feature from JSON params over a `Fixture` without a timeline (`params`, `params_with`, `fixture`, `build`, `updates`, `update`, `updated_shape`, `assert_volume`, `assert_bounds`, `python`, `python_with`). Unit tests cover what a feature computes on its own; whole documents of built-in features are tested in `partsmith-recompute`.
- `tests/common/mod.rs` has the `Probe` and `Slab` test features, `ContextFixture` (with the writer chains of `BuildInputs::history`), `built_body`, which builds a solid record of the built-in registry (a box or a cylinder of the feature specs) into a body state, `NoNames` and `LabelNames` (fixed labels for hand-built messages) render helpers that make a `RenderContext`, `pointer` (a warning's field pointer), the fix builders `use_reference`, `pick` and `keep_and_pick` (the fixes of a followed reference), `placed` and `fallback` (the placement or the fallback a test expects of a `face_placement` result, `FacePlacementResult`), and `moved_state` with `NO_TURN` and `HALF_TURN_ABOUT_Z` (a state as a Move body leaves it). `tests/common/timeline.rs` builds built-in records in timeline order the way recompute does (states, writer chains and their `writers`, outputs, no cache or diagnosis), so build context and message tests read real body states (`run_with_parameters` adds document parameters, which builds and `Names::value` read), render with the document's names and pass the `Diagnosis` a scenario implies (`with_names` gives those names with the run's outputs, `with_end_context` the render context of a suppressed feature over the end states, for references resolved through `fixture`); `tests/common/records.rs` writes the specs' shared setups (`BLOCK`, `SLOT(a, b)`, `CUBE10`, `CUBE20`, `BOX-BELOW`, the L-shaped body of features/fillet.md test 4 with its merged top and front top edge, the setups of corpus R32b, R33 and R38, and the newcomer corpus's `STEP` with `RISER_EDGE` and `rounded_box` for the "too large" searches), features/sketch.md's `FACE-SK2`, the Move body by (100, 0, 0), and the stored references that several tests pick (`RIGHT_TOP_EDGE`, `FRONT_TOP_EDGE`, `RIGHT_SIDE`, `BLOCK_TOP`, `LEFT_BOTTOM_EDGE`, `ATTACHED_BOX_TOP`); section 6.3 tests 27 to 29 (coplanar and collinear references, axis sides, face placements with frames) are in `tests/context_lookups.rs`, test 30 (placements before a removal) and the motions of Move body and copies in `tests/context_states.rs`, and the phase 2 message rows (`not_coplanar`, `not_collinear`, `sketch_turned_over`, a `plane_fallback` placed before the removal) in `tests/messages.rs`; the phase 3 rows are in `tests/error_messages.rs` (sections 4.1 and 4.3, the "too large" limits and their fixes), `tests/warning_messages.rs` (section 4.2 and every `NoEffectHow` of section 3.15) and `tests/body_rules.rs` (`apply_op` with a `Direction`, `reverse_hits`, `separate_solid` and `closed_void`); `tests/common/lint.rs` is the message lint of the error guidelines. `tests/descriptions.rs` checks one rule of section 3.12 per test beyond section 6.5 test 1 (with `describe_before` for a state just before a feature), and `tests/followed_messages.rs` the exact texts of `reference_followed` and `reference_widened` for each follow, with and without `Diagnosis::by`.
- `tests/budgets.rs` times the framework budgets of features.md section 5 with `std::time::Instant` in release builds (all eight, the `BodyState::elements` budget of a 200-face body, the `describe_elements` budget of five pocket edges after 100 records and the `face_placement_before_removal` budget of a 50-writer history included, are skipped in debug builds).

## Built-in features

Each type lives in `src/builtin/<type>.rs` (`hole` in `src/builtin/hole/`) and is registered by adding `Registry::register::<Type>` to `BUILTINS` in `src/builtin/mod.rs`, which also holds the type groups element descriptions read (`PRIMITIVE_TYPES`, `CUTTING_TYPES`, `COPYING_TYPES`), built from the types' `TYPE` constants. All 20 types of features.md section 3.13 are registered, so the acceptance tests that need them run by default.

Registered types:

- Primitives (features.md section 3.14): `box` (`BoxFeature`, since `Box` is the standard library's pointer), `cylinder` (`Cylinder`), `sphere` (`Sphere`), `cone` (`Cone`), `torus` (`Torus`). Each params struct puts its sizes first, then the shared `plane` and `position` (`PrimitivePlacement`), then `op`; sizes are checked in field order before any kernel work by the positive length rule of features.md section 3.4 (`PrimitiveParams::positive_sizes`, through `common::positive_length`: below `LINEAR_TOLERANCE`, 1e-6 mm, a size is `OutOfRange` (`GreaterThan(0)`) like 0; a torus also needs its ring at least that much wider than its tube), faces are named `<feature>/<role>` from the kernel's `PrimitiveFace` roles (`common::primitive_tool`), and the tool goes through `apply_op`. The box shifts the kernel's corner-anchored frame by half its length and width; a cone treats a radius below `LINEAR_TOLERANCE` (1e-6 mm) as an apex and builds a kernel cylinder when its radii differ by less than that.
- Sketch-based features (features/sketch.md, datum_plane.md, extrude.md, revolve.md, hole.md): `sketch` (`SketchFeature`, params `partsmith_sketch::SketchParams`, no Python hook because the engine writes sketch blocks), `datum_plane` (`DatumPlane`), `extrude` (`Extrude`, with `ExtrudeExtent`), `revolve` (`Revolve`, with `RevolveExtent`) and `hole` (`Hole`, with `HoleType` and `HoleExtent`). A sketch on a face is placed by the first of features/sketch.md section 3.3's steps that applies: `BuildContext::face_placement` with its stored fallback as the hint in its `fallback_frame`, reporting the face reference's warnings and notices at `plane.face` and `SketchTurnedOver` when the face turned over; for a missing face, `face_placement_before_removal` with a `PlaneFallback` placed `BeforeRemoval`; else the fallback, moved by the face's motion for `fallback_frame: body`, with a `PlaneFallback` placed `Saved` (each with the `FaceFallback`'s cause and loss). Its motion is the face's for `fallback_frame: body` and the datum's for a datum plane; it evaluates only driving dimensions and maps the solver's result to `SketchInvalid`, `SketchConflict`, `SketchNotSolved` or a `RedundantConstraints` warning. `datum_plane` passes on the identity for an origin base, the base datum's motion, and for a face base the rigid motion from the face placement rule on the face mapped back by its motion to the placement (`base_motion`, the identity when the face has not moved or the frame does not follow it). Extrude and revolve sweep `ResolvedProfile::faces()` through `common::swept_tool`, which names the tool with `sweep_tool` (the profile's successions as lateral successions, the sketch's motion), then apply the op after the profile's warnings and notices (`ResolvedProfile::into_messages`); a cut extrude records an end cap its result lacks as absent (`Through` without a start cap in the result, else `EndOutside`), and a full revolve both caps (`FullTurn`); through all goes 1 mm past the farthest bounding box corner along the direction (`common::farthest_corner` and `common::THROUGH_ALL_MARGIN`, shared with hole, decision D21). Every length they check uses the positive length rule (`common::positive_length` and `is_positive_length`), extrude's two sides included (a sum below `LINEAR_TOLERANCE` is `BothSidesZero`). Revolve checks that the axis lies in the sketch plane and that the profile stays on one side of it on the sketch regions, before any kernel work, treats an angle at most 1e-9 rad as out of range like 0 and one within 1e-9 rad of 360 deg as a full turn. Hole lives in `builtin/hole/`: `mod.rs` holds the feature and its build steps (`distinct_points`, `drill_candidates`, `finish`), `size.rs` the checked sizes and the half cross-section, `tool.rs` the segment roles (their discriminants are the curve tags) and the tool, and `overlap.rs` the overlap decision (`cut_candidates`, `tools_with_own_faces`, `shared_volumes`). It evaluates and checks the diameter (its radius at least `LINEAR_TOLERANCE`, since the entry and bottom segments are that long), then the depth, then the head (a head diameter at least D + 2 `LINEAR_TOLERANCE`, a counterbore depth at least `LINEAR_TOLERANCE`), then the tip, and requires a head to end at least `LINEAR_TOLERANCE` above the shoulder (`ends_above_shoulder`), reporting the bounds the user typed (the depth, or the head diameter whose cone ends at it). It builds one tool per distinct point by revolving a half cross-section in the plane of the sketch x axis and the drilling direction, its segments tagged by role (`entry`, `counterbore`, `counterbore-floor`, `countersink`, `bore`, `bottom`, `tip`, and the axis segment, which sweeps no face), its faces with the point sketch's `SketchResult::motion` (step 7); a point whose tool does not overlap the body gets `HoleMissesBody`. The body's bounding box is read once; a tool whose box misses it does not overlap, and the others are cut from the body with one kernel `cut` (`cut_candidates`). A cut that leaves the volume unchanged means no tool overlaps; otherwise a tool overlaps when the cut's history has a result face from one of its faces and from no body face (`tools_with_own_faces`), and a tool without one (it misses, touches or swallows whole solids) is decided by its shared volume (`shared_volumes`: one kernel `common` with the compound of such tools when their boxes are more than `LINEAR_TOLERANCE` apart, each solid of the result counting for the tool whose box is nearest to its center, else one `common` per tool). When every cut tool leaves a face of its own, the cut goes straight through `apply_op_result` (whose `NoEffect` is the unchanged volume) and is the result, so the usual hole costs one boolean on the body; otherwise the cut's volume and the shared volumes decide the other tools before anything is named, the cut goes through `apply_op_result` only when every tool overlaps, and else the overlapping tools are cut again with `apply_op`, both with the drilling direction and the point sketch. A point the sketch no longer has is skipped with `PointMissing` (none left is `UnknownEntity` of the first), a point whose tool misses the body gets `HoleMissesBody` with its sketch coordinates and, on that warning path only, `reverse_hits` from its tool drilled the other way, and `NoEffect` (`Missed`) follows only when Points holds two or more entries. A through-all hole is max(F, h) + 1 mm long, with F the farthest corner distance and h the head depth, so a head deeper than the body still leaves a bore below it. Each overlapping tool whose end face (`bottom`, or `tip` with a tip) the result lacks is recorded absent (`ToolEnds`, `absent_ends`): `Through` when its `entry` is gone too, else `EndOutside`.
- Dress-up (features/fillet.md, chamfer.md, shell.md): `fillet` (`Fillet`), `chamfer` (`Chamfer`, with `ChamferDistance` tagged `equal` or `two_distances`) and `shell` (`Shell`). They share `dressup.rs`: fillet and chamfer call its steps in order (`select_edges`, `no_effect` when no edge is left, `sorted_by_name`, `run_or_search`, then `finish_blend`, which names the result and makes the update); `select_edges` resolves `edges` and skips smooth edges with an `Ignored` warning (fewer than two faces in the body state's topology, as at a seam, or two faces that kernel `Shape::edge_normals` records as `G1OrBetter` or whose outward normals at the edge's middle differ by at most `SMOOTH_ANGLE`, 1e-3 rad, on every surface type; a knife edge, with opposite normals, and an edge the query rejects as invalid input, where a face has no defined normal, have a corner), `run_or_search` calls the kernel and runs the "too large" search on any failure except invalid input and internal errors, measuring on that failure path only (`face_limit`: the narrowest face next to a selected edge by the kernel's `width_across`, ties to the names that sort first, and one more attempt without that edge's reference for `removable`; the shell's `round_faces`), an edge selected for a reference that resolved to it alone names that entry in its `Ignored` warning, `no_effect` adds `NoEffect` (`Skipped`) only when Edges holds two or more entries, a shell that would remove every face of a solid is `ShellRemovesAll` before any kernel work, and `add_origins` turns kernel history into the face origins that `name_blend` and the shell's naming map to names (naming.md sections 3.6.5 to 3.6.7), and `modified` makes the update, which carries the topology the naming read. Fillet and chamfer pass their edges to the kernel sorted by element name in byte order (`dressup::sorted_by_name`), so the order of `edges` never matters (the kernel's blend surfaces, down to the last bits, and a chamfer's side of a tangent chain depend on the order it gets edges in), and a two-distance chamfer measures `expr` on the first face of each edge (`flip` swaps the distances): the adjacent face in the family of the first face term of the stored reference the edge was resolved from (`SelectedEdge::source`, `NameSet::family` with its merged names), else the one whose name sorts first, with a `Related` `ReferenceFollowed` notice when the resolution said nothing about that entry (features/chamfer.md section 3.3 step 4, corpus R22). Shell hollows each solid of the body with its own removed faces (a solid without any becomes a closed hollow) and combines several results with the kernel's `compound`, mapping each solid's history through `Shape::locate`.
- Body features (features/boolean.md, mirror.md, linear_pattern.md, circular_pattern.md, move_body.md, split_body.md, import.md): `boolean` (`Boolean`, with `BooleanOp`), `mirror` (`Mirror`), `linear_pattern` (`LinearPattern`, with `LinearPatternDirection`), `circular_pattern` (`CircularPattern`), `move_body` (`MoveBody`, with `MoveBodyRotation`), `split_body` (`SplitBody`) and `import` (`Import`). `boolean` checks the target and every tool body for a solid, then calls `apply_op` with one `Tool` per tool body (its shape and a copy of its naming map), which makes one kernel `fuse`, `cut` or `common` call; consumed tools get `Clear` updates even when the operation had no effect. The copy features share `copy.rs`: `validate_target` and `add_source`/`add_target` for `op` and `target`, `op_hint`, `check_instance_limit` (at most 10000 instances, the original included; patterns read their counts with `eval_count(..., 1)`, and check `common::check_canceled` between copies), and `Source` (`new` checks the source holds a solid), whose `copy` transforms the source and names it with `NamingMap::with_instance` and the copy's transform as the motion (`common::naming_motion`) and whose `place` combines every copy with one `apply_op` (join into the source, new into the target). With op new a target that holds a solid is `BodyNotEmpty` at any count, with or without copies; without copies (count 1) the source keeps an unchanged `Join` update, or the still empty target gets none, with a `NoEffect` warning. `move_body` carries the input naming map over with every name, `moved` by its transform (`common::naming_motion`), and reports `NoEffect` with an unchanged `Modify` update when nothing moves. `split_body` needs a solid above `VOLUME_TOLERANCE` of the body's volume on each side, names the whole kernel split once (section faces by side, other faces by history) and gives each body the projection of that map onto its solids through `Shape::locate`. `import` names every face `<id>/face` (numbered `#n` in geometric order) and has no dedicated Python form. Nested param structs use `deny_unknown_fields` so an unknown key inside them is reported instead of dropped.

## Rules

- Built-in features use only the public API, like a plugin would (features.md section 2.11): `?` on kernel and naming calls (`From<KernelError>`, `From<NamingError>`), `FeatureError::internal`, `point`, `vector`, `BuildContext::face_placement` and `face_placement_before_removal`, `PlacementExt::moved`, `VOLUME_TOLERANCE`, `apply_op_result`, `Measurements`, `BodyState::measurements` and the naming bridge are public for that, and items of private modules are imported from the crate root (`crate::name_shape`, `crate::PrimitivePlacement`). `builtin/common.rs` holds what the built-ins share and uses only public items, including `positive_length` and `is_positive_length` (the positive length rule of section 3.4, which fillet, chamfer and shell use too), `length_in_range` and `angle_in_range` (a value checked right after it is evaluated), `out_of_range` (an `OutOfRange` whose message bound differs from the rule's), `require_selection` (`EmptySelection` in `validate`), `check_canceled`, `three_lengths`, `single_or_compound`, `swept_tool`, `target_hint`, `result_faces` and `has_face` (the faces a cut extrude or hole left, for the faces it records absent), `unchanged` (an update that keeps a body's state and its measurements), `naming_motion` (a kernel transform as the naming motion that copies and moves give their faces) and `name_on_body` (names the shape a fillet, chamfer, shell or split body made from a body, its new faces with the base motion of the body's naming map, the motion of a name it never saw, section 3.9). The framework's own long steps use the crate-private `BuildContext::check_canceled`.
- Every registered feature needs an example in `examples/`.
- `FeatureError` keeps the payload types the spec gives, so the crate expects `clippy::result_large_err` (see `lib.rs`).
