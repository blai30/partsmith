# 0058: The sketch solver's trust region takes the exact step, from a radius the first damped step sets

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05). It settles option (d) of ADR 0052's step rank test, the exact trust-region step, which the owner scheduled right after M1 on 2026-11-04 (ADR 0052's status). Each choice below is settled by the owner's rule. It amends ADR 0052 decision 3: the second pass and Levenberg-Marquardt now follow the trust region with the exact step, not dog-leg. It amends ADR 0004's "dog-leg trust region method": the trust region keeps its gain ratio and its stop rules and takes the exact step in place of dog-leg's blend. ADR 0052's decisions 1, 2 and 4 stand. No schema, protocol, public type, license or dependency changes. The method's constants (the first damping 1e-3, the floor 1e-10, the band of 10 percent and the limit of 10 iterations) are private constants of `src/solver/numeric.rs`, like `STEP_RANK_TOLERANCE` and the trust radius floor (ADR 0052 decision 1). No public constant is added. The change is inside `partsmith-sketch`'s solver (sketch.md 3.7.4) and the doc comments of `MAX_ITERATIONS` and `RANK_TOLERANCE`. Nothing in it is a change that AGENTS.md sends to human review. Choice 9 changes the bound of the M2 plan's gate 8 seed check. That change goes to human review at the M2 gate (plan gate 19). The examples whose results move are re-blessed once, after the libm perturbation check, in a dated entry of this ADR (M2 plan gate 5, the section "Re-blessed examples" below).

## Context

- **Slides that remained.** ADR 0052 decision 1 stopped the slides through a row accepted at rounding size. Slides through a row that repeats others already at the warm start stayed (sketch.md open issue 21). On the solver of a3f4573, seeds 230290, 259081, 478151, 505952 and 742663 of the generator (sketch.md 6.13) end 0.12 to 0.27 mm from their known solution. Seed 9841460 stretches a free line by 460 mm and ends 262 mm from its known solution. Seed 5635218625053008121 slides a free end 5.36 mm. The moved warm starts of these two solve 1.0e-6 and 1.2e-6 away from the moved results. Of seeds 1,000,000 to 1,999,999, seven move a coordinate more than 0.1 mm from their warm starts: seeds 1036660, 1071907, 1228333, 1538706, 1583858, 1705412 and 1735421 move one 4.76, 0.88, 0.17, 0.16, 0.10, 0.39 and 0.17 mm (measured 2026-11-07). Test 192 fails on such a slide about once in 2,000 to 5,000 nightly runs. The owner accepted that rate for M1 and scheduled option (d) right after M1 (2026-11-04).
- **What slides.** The repeating row lies 1e-5 to 1e-4 outside the span of the rows before it at the warm start. The Gauss-Newton step divides the row's residual by that part. In seed 9841460 the first Gauss-Newton step is 742 mm long. Dog-leg starts its radius at that length and follows the step. Any trust region whose first step is the full Gauss-Newton step slides the same way.
- **The exact step.** The minimizer of the Gauss-Newton model within the trust radius is the damped step `h(mu) = -(J^T J + mu I)^-1 J^T r` whose length is the radius (Moré, "The Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm: implementation and theory", 1978, the method of MINPACK's `lmder` and `lmpar`). Damping scales the part of the step along a direction of singular value `s` by `s / (s^2 + mu)` in place of `1 / s`. While `mu` is much larger than `s^2`, a direction that only a repeating row sets hardly moves.
- **Measured with a prototype** (2026-11-07, a scratch copy of the crate at a3f4573 on the 4-core development container, deleted afterwards). Each finding below decided a choice.
  - *The first radius sets the slide.* With the exact step and dog-leg's first radius, `norm(h_gn)`, the seven seeds of sketch.md open issue 21 above slide as before. With a first radius equal to the length of the damped step at Levenberg-Marquardt's first damping, `1e-3 * max(diag(J^T J))`, each of them ends within 0.015 of its known solution and 0.014 of its warm start. The moved warm starts of 9841460 and 5635218625053008121 then solve within 7e-14 of the moved results.
  - *The model the damped step minimizes.* A damped step over only the rows the step's rank test accepts failed both passes on 2 of the 975,828 generated cases of seeds 0 to 999,999 (seeds 37618 and 637669, each with a dimension repeated). The gain ratio measures the model over every row. A repeated row counts once in such a step and as often as it is repeated in the ratio. Every damped step there predicted an increase, and the radius shrank until the iterations stopped. Levenberg-Marquardt converged on both. A damped step over every row converges in the first pass on all 975,828 cases.
  - *A floor on the damping.* Without a lower bound on `mu`, the exact step can fill the radius along a direction that only a repeating row sets, with `mu` near that direction's `s^2`. Within the first 15 runs of the nightly command, test 192 failed on seed 12075137138286841106 with the offset (0, 6.013428068696318). Moré's iteration stopped at `mu = 3.35e-12` in the original solve and `3.43e-12` in the moved one, both within 10 percent of the radius. The results ended 2.4e-5 apart. With `mu` held at least `1e-10 * max(diag(J^T J))` they agree within 3e-14, and 400 runs of the nightly command (4096 cases each) passed.
  - *The factorization.* Each `mu` needs a factorization of its own. A Householder QR of the augmented rows `[J, sqrt(mu) I]` with the Gauss-Newton step's sparse QR stored 68,394 reflector entries on the chain of sketch.md 5.2, against 6,556 for the Gauss-Newton step. With this QR a chain drag frame took 14 ms. A QR of `[J; sqrt(mu) I]` by columns, the way Levenberg-Marquardt computes its step today, took 25 ms a frame. A sparse Cholesky factorization of `J J^T + mu I` in row order has 5,670 entries below its diagonal, as many as the Gauss-Newton step's `R` has above its diagonal. With it a frame took a median of 2.69 to 2.88 ms (budget 4 ms).

## Decision

1. **The exact step** (sketch.md 3.7.4). The trust region keeps its model `L(h) = 0.5 * norm(r + J h)^2`, its gain ratio, its acceptance rule (`rho > 0`), `MAX_ITERATIONS` and its stop rules. Its step is the minimizer of `L` within `norm(h) <= Delta`, found by Moré's iteration after MINPACK's `lmpar`. The step is the Gauss-Newton step when that step is at most 1.1 times the radius. Otherwise it is the damped step `h(mu)`, with `mu` from Newton's method on `1 / norm(h(mu)) - 1 / Delta`. Newton's method stops when the length is within 10 percent of the radius or after 10 iterations. The Gauss-Newton step keeps ADR 0052's rank tolerances: the step tolerance in the first pass, `RANK_TOLERANCE` in the second.
2. **Every row in the damped step.** `h(mu)` minimizes the model over every row the component solves, the rows the rank test leaves out of the Gauss-Newton step included. It is the model the gain ratio measures.
3. **A floor on the damping.** Moré's iteration keeps `mu` at least `1e-10 * max(diag(J^T J))` at the current point. A step that the floor keeps shorter than the radius is taken as it is.
4. **The factorization.** `h(mu) = -J^T y`, with `y` the solution of `(J J^T + mu I) y = r` from a sparse Cholesky factorization that eliminates the rows in the component's row order. In exact arithmetic every pivot is at least `mu`, and the floor keeps `mu` far above rounding. The damped step steers the path only: convergence is judged on the residuals, and the refinement of section 3.7.4 uses the Gauss-Newton step's QR.
5. **The first radius** is the length of `h(mu0)` at the start point, with `mu0 = 1e-3 * max(diag(J^T J))`, the damping Levenberg-Marquardt starts from in section 3.7.4 (at least 1e-6, the floor dog-leg had). The first step is the Gauss-Newton step when it is at most 1.1 times that length, else `h(mu0)`.
6. **The radius follows the steps** (MINPACK). After a step with `rho < 0.25`, the radius becomes `0.5 * min(Delta, 10 * norm(h))`, and the next iteration of Moré's starts from twice the damping used. After a Gauss-Newton step or a step with `rho >= 0.75`, the radius becomes `2 * norm(h)`, and it starts from half the damping. Otherwise both stay.
7. **What stays from ADR 0052.** The step tolerance of decision 1 ranks the Gauss-Newton steps of the first pass. Decision 3's second pass runs the same trust region from the same start with `RANK_TOLERANCE` when the first pass does not converge (test 258 still needs it). Levenberg-Marquardt stays the last pass, unchanged. Decision 2's second solve of a drag frame without the weight stays: the exact step does not converge on test 257's weighted frame either. Decision 4's fold rule stays: the exact step minimizes the same Gauss-Newton model, which is flat at a fold.
8. **Tests** (sketch.md section 6). Test 259 pins section 3.11's minimum-norm promise on ten replayable seeds. Property test 260 holds every generated case to it. Test 336 holds the seven slides of seeds 1,000,000 to 1,999,999 to test 260's bound. Test 261 replays the translated cases of seeds 9841460, 5635218625053008121 and 12075137138286841106. Test 262 replays the repeated dimensions of seeds 37618 and 637669. Test 258 now covers the second pass of the exact step. Test 255's literals of the chain drag are pasted again (sketch.md 6.22). The ignored test `scan_seeds` of sketch.md 6.13 runs the M2 plan's gate 8 scan.
9. **Re-blessed examples.** The implementation makes the list once, after the libm perturbation check, by the procedure in the section below, and records it in a dated entry there.

## Options considered

Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *The first radius.* The options: (a) `norm(h_gn)`, dog-leg's first radius; (b) the length of `h(mu0)` at Levenberg-Marquardt's first damping; (c) MINPACK's default, 100 times the norm of the scaled variables; (d) the length of the Cauchy step. The rule takes (b), the safest. Under (a) the seeds slide as they did with dog-leg (measured). Under (c) the first step is the full Gauss-Newton step too: 100 times the norm of a sketch's coordinates is far longer than the 742 mm of seed 9841460's first step. The Cauchy step of (d) is shorter than the Gauss-Newton step on almost every sketch whose directions are not equally stiff. It would damp the first step of nearly every solve. (b)'s damping is the standard start of Levenberg-Marquardt (`tau = 1e-3` in Madsen, Nielsen and Tingleff, 2004), the one section 3.7.4 already uses.
2. *How the subproblem is solved.* The options: (a) Moré's iteration with MINPACK's band of 10 percent and at most 10 iterations; (b) Newton's method on `mu` until rounding stops it; (c) Steihaug's truncated conjugate gradients; (d) bisection on `mu`. The rule takes (a), the industry standard: MINPACK's `lmpar`, which SciPy's `leastsq` and Eigen's `LevenbergMarquardt` also run. (b) costs more factorizations a step. With the floor of choice 4, (a) already makes the translated solves of test 261 agree within 7e-14. (c) does not give the exact step that option (d) of ADR 0052 names. (d) converges slowly and needs more factorizations than Newton's method.
3. *The rows of the damped step.* The options: (a) every row; (b) the rows the rank test accepts. The rule takes (a), the safest. (b) failed both passes on seeds 37618 and 637669, whose damped steps the gain ratio over every row rejected.
4. *The floor on the damping.* The options: (a) none, MINPACK's lower bound 0; (b) `1e-12 * max(diag(J^T J))`; (c) `1e-10 * max(diag(J^T J))`; (d) `1e-8 * max(diag(J^T J))`. On the translated case of seed 12075137138286841106 the results ended 2.4e-5 apart with (a), 6.3e-13 with (b) and 2.8e-14 with (c) and (d). The ten seeds of test 259 end the same under (b), (c) and (d). The rule takes (c), the safest. The floor is a hundred times the square of the step tolerance, in units of `J`'s largest squared column norm. A direction whose singular value is below the step tolerance in those units moves at most a hundredth as far as it would undamped. Every pivot of the factorization stays six orders above rounding. (d) also halves the steps along directions whose singular values are up to a hundred times the step tolerance.
5. *The damped step's factorization.* The options: (a) a sparse Cholesky factorization of `J J^T + mu I` in row order; (b) a Householder QR of the augmented rows `[J, sqrt(mu) I]` with the Gauss-Newton step's code; (c) a QR of `[J; sqrt(mu) I]` by columns, as Levenberg-Marquardt computes its step; (d) Givens rotations of `sqrt(mu) I` into the Gauss-Newton step's `R`, as MINPACK's `qrsolv` does with its `R`; (e) a sparse Cholesky factorization from a library, such as `faer`'s (MIT), the library ADR 0015 set aside for the QR. The rule takes (a), the safest for the drag budget of sketch.md 5.1: 2.69 to 2.88 ms a chain frame, against 14 ms for (b) and 25 ms for (c). (d) was not built. It needs `R` by rows, and each added row sweeps the rows of `R` after it. (e) is as open as (a). The rule does not take it: (a) is the safer one, with no new dependency, no license review at the gate, and an elimination in the row order whose plans the Gauss-Newton factorization already reuses (ADR 0015). (a) is the regularized normal equations written over the rows, `-(J^T J + mu I)^-1 J^T r = -J^T (J J^T + mu I)^-1 r`, the damped least-squares step of inverse kinematics (Wampler, 1986; Nakamura and Hanafusa, 1986). The Ceres Solver's `SPARSE_NORMAL_CHOLESKY` factors the same equations over the variables, `J^T J + D^T D`, by sparse Cholesky. Over the rows, the matrix keeps the row order and the pattern of the Gauss-Newton factor (ADR 0015). Forming `J J^T` gives up the QR's accuracy, which the Gauss-Newton step keeps. The damped step needs it less: the floor bounds its matrix's condition, and the step only steers the path.
6. *The radius update.* The options: (a) MINPACK's update of decision 6; (b) dog-leg's, `Delta = max(Delta, 3 * norm(h))` above 0.75 and halving below 0.25. Both gave the same results on the seeds and the crate's tests. The rule takes (a), the industry standard for the exact step. It is also the safest: the radius follows the length of the steps, and a late step cannot reach far along a direction that only a repeating row sets.
7. *The passes after the first.* The options: (a) keep the second pass with `RANK_TOLERANCE` and Levenberg-Marquardt; (b) drop the second pass; (c) drop Levenberg-Marquardt. Without the second pass, test 258 fails. No case of the 1,951,609 generated cases of seeds 0 to 1,999,999 needed the second pass or Levenberg-Marquardt. The rule takes (a), the safest. Both passes cost only the solves whose first pass fails.
8. *How the promise is pinned.* The options: (a) a test that replays the seeds and a property test over the generator; (b) the replay test alone, the minimum the M2 plan asks (gate 8); (c) the property test alone. The rule takes (a), the safest. The seeds fail on every target the moment a change brings their slides back. The property finds a slide on a case nobody recorded. Its bound, 0.1 mm from the warm start, is ten times the warm start's noise, the factor the generator's discard rules use. The prototype's largest move over 1,951,609 cases was 0.031 mm (seed 1036660). The solver of a3f4573 moves the five seeds of sketch.md open issue 21 0.13 to 0.26 mm from their warm starts, and the seven of seeds 1,000,000 to 1,999,999 0.10 to 4.76 mm. Those seven are replayed too, by test 336 with the property's bound: the warm start of seed 1036660 is 0.018 mm from its known solution, and its result moves 0.031 mm, more than test 259's 0.02.
9. *The bound of the replay test.* The options: (a) gate 8's 0.01 from the known solution; (b) 0.02 from both the warm start and the known solution; (c) test 260's 0.1 from the warm start. Under (a) the test fails with the exact step. With the prototype, seeds 230290 and 505952 end 0.0149 and 0.0115 from their known solutions. The warm starts of those two are already 0.0154 and 0.0111 from theirs. The rule takes (b), the safest bound that holds. It holds each result to both points, with a margin over the measured 0.015. (c) is the property's bound for cases nobody recorded. On the recorded seeds it would pass a slide of up to 0.1 mm, seven times what the exact step moves them (0.014). At the gate, test 259 stands for gate 8's seed check.
10. *The order of the factorization's arithmetic* (2026-11-07, wave 1's implementation). Decision 4 fixes the factor and the row order. It leaves open the order of the operations, and that order decides the last bits of `C`. The options: (a) up-looking, one row of `C` at a time, as CSparse's `cs_chol` (Davis, "Direct Methods for Sparse Linear Systems", 2006), with the entries of each row in ascending column order (`cs_chol` takes them in the order `cs_ereach` returns); (b) left-looking, one column at a time; (c) supernodal or multifrontal. The rule takes (a), the industry standard for sparse matrices of this size and the simplest. It eliminates the rows in their order, and its sums run in the same fixed order on every target.
