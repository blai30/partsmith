# 0068: A model corpus and a naming metric scored against an oracle the test author writes

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05). It builds the M2 plan's item 9 and records its choice 24, option (a): the corpus is made of models written for the project, under Apache-2.0 like the examples. Each choice below is settled by the owner's rule: the most open-source approach, then the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. It changes no file format, protocol schema, naming algorithm or license, and adds no third-party dependency. The corpus is under the license ADR 0028 already gives the test corpus. The runner's one new development dependency is the workspace crate `partsmith-naming`, for the tests of `partsmith-cli`. A development dependency is not an edge of architecture.md's crate graph. Nothing here goes to the human review of the M2 gate (AGENTS.md Process, m2-plan.md gate 19). [corpus.md](../spec/corpus.md) specifies it, with the changes to [delivery.md](../spec/delivery.md) section 2.8 and [naming.md](../spec/naming.md) open issue 6.

## Context

- **What the design asks.** Technical design section 1 sets a v1 success criterion: fewer than 1 percent of upstream edits in the regression corpus break a downstream reference. Section 16 asks for a corpus of 500 models at launch, rebuilt on every merge against stored volume, surface area, bounding box and face count. It also asks for scripted edits (a sketch dimension, a feature added upstream, a reorder) whose metric is the share of downstream references that still resolve to the correct element, above 99 percent. Section 17 asks for oracles agents can trust, a held-out corpus and differential build123d twins. delivery.md 1.2 left all of it for later.
- **What the plan asks.** Item 9: the oracle is written by hand. With each model and each scripted edit, the test author writes the expected element name of every downstream reference, or "lost". The metric counts matches against those names. The stored baselines only track drift between runs. It runs over the examples, the four success parts and a corpus that starts at 100 project-written models, rebuilt on every merge. Every M2 naming rule is measured as it lands. Wave 1 lands the harness, the runner and the baselines. Wave 5 grows the corpus to 100 and holds the metric against the 99 percent target (gate 6). Gate 4 asks that a rebuild after each scripted edit leaves the saved text byte-identical. Choice 24 picked option (a) for the corpus's license.
- **The examples runner** (`crates/partsmith-cli/tests/examples.rs`, with `tests/examples/export.rs` and `tests/examples/coverage.rs`) checks the layout, canonical form and coverage of `examples/`. It rebuilds each example with `partsmith rebuild --checks --json` and compares it with its `.expected.json`, in parallel. `PARTSMITH_BLESS=1` regenerates the expected results, and the runner refuses that under `CI`. Its helpers `Problems`, `compare`, `bless_mode` and `announce` are private to the module and its submodules. `ci.yml`'s `rust` job runs it in `cargo test --workspace`. `rust-platforms` runs it on `windows-2025` and `macos-15` with `cargo test -p partsmith-cli --test integration examples::` (ci.yml at a3f4573).
- **The examples at a3f4573.** 294 examples. 97 of them outside `examples/bench/` store element references (objects with a `q` member), 181 in all. The benchmark stores 58.
- **What the engine offers** (engine.md 3.5 and 3.9). Inside a transaction, `set_focus` on a feature makes the display state the state just before it, the one the feature resolves its references against. `resolve_references` resolves `{body, reference}` pairs with partsmith-naming's resolver against the element set of that state and returns a status (`resolved`, `ambiguous`, `missing`) and matches. In a headless session a match's index is the element's position among its kind in `list_elements`, whose descriptors carry the names. `crates/partsmith-cli/tests/corpus/mod.rs` already picks references this way, focus by focus, for cli.md's tests 55 to 62. The protocol's `Resolution` carries no `MatchMethod`. No command resolves a stored sketch region.
- **Where references sit.** A stored reference is a JSON object with a `q` member, as `list_uses` reads them (engine.md 3.24). It sits next to a `body` member (spec README decision D2). In the `.ptsm` schema at a3f4573, 16 of the 20 feature types can hold one (`AxisRef`, `PlaneRef`, `SketchPlane`, and the `edges` and `remove_faces` lists). `boolean`, `extrude`, `hole` and `import` cannot.
- **Parameters.** `Registry::dependencies` lists every expression of a record (features.md section 2). `Expr::references` names the parameters an expression uses (expressions.md section 2). Both are public.
- **Measured on 2026-11-07** on the 4-core development container: a debug `partsmith rebuild --json` took 0.06 to 0.66 s for four examples and 3.6 s for the 50-feature benchmark.

## Decision

1. **The spec.** A new spec, `docs/spec/corpus.md`, holds the corpus layout, the scripted edits and the oracle, the downstream rule, the metric, the stored outcomes and the drift rule, the first 100 models and the checks. delivery.md 2.8 names the two new companions of an example and points there. delivery.md 3.7 and 1.3 say how CI runs it.
2. **The corpus.** `corpus/<category>/<stem>.ptsm` with `<stem>.expected.json` (feature statuses and each body's volume, area, bounding box and face count), `<stem>.edits.json` (the scripts and the oracle, written by hand) and `<stem>.outcomes.json` (the stored outcomes, written by the bless), and `assets/` under the examples' rule. 100 models in eleven categories, 46 of them in wave 1 with M1's features and the rest with the features of waves 2 to 4. A corpus model is a whole part: at least 5 features and 2 stored references.
3. **Scripted edits.** A script is a named list of edits in `apply_edits`'s JSON form, of seven types: `update_parameter`, `add_parameter`, `add_body`, `add_feature`, `update_feature`, `remove_feature` and `move_feature`. Every example outside the benchmark that stores a reference carries at least one script. A corpus model and each of the four success parts carry at least three, one an update and one an insertion.
4. **The oracle.** For each script, `expect` maps a feature id and a reference's JSON Pointer within the record to `"lost"`, one element name or a list of the pieces of a split. It names exactly the script's downstream references. The test author writes it from the model, the script and naming.md, by the user's intent, without running the implementation.
5. **Downstream.** A feature of the edited document is downstream when it was in the base document, is active, and the state before it may have changed: the records before it differ, labels aside, or one of them uses a changed parameter. Its stored references are the downstream references.
6. **Resolution.** The runner opens the edited document in a new headless session, cold, focuses each downstream feature, resolves its references with `resolve_references` and names the matches from `list_elements`. The same open checks gate 4: the edit changes no record it does not name, and a rebuild saves the edited text again byte for byte.
7. **The metric.** A reference matches when its outcome is the expected element set, or is `missing` or `ambiguous` where the author wrote `"lost"`. The metric is the matches over every scored reference of the examples and the corpus, pooled. Check 8 prints it on every run with its mismatches and the longest element name. The target, above 99 percent, is judged at the M2 gate.
8. **Drift.** Each outcome is stored. Every run compares the computed outcomes with the stored ones. A difference fails until a bless rewrites the file and a reviewer sees it in the pull request. No run writes an oracle.
9. **CI.** Nine checks in two submodules of the examples runner, `examples::model_corpus` and `examples::naming_metric`. They run in `rust` and in `rust-platforms` on every change, with no workflow change.

## Alternatives considered

The plan settled the corpus's license (choice 24): (a) models written for the project under Apache-2.0, like the examples; (b) models under CC0, or community models under other licenses. It picked (a). ADR 0028 sets one license for the code, the examples and the test corpus. This record does not reopen it.

The choices below are each Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *Where the spec lives.* (i) A new spec, `docs/spec/corpus.md`; (ii) a new section of delivery.md beside the examples corpus; (iii) a section of naming.md. All three are equally open. The rule takes (i), the safest. The corpus is an area with its own files, formats, checks, messages and acceptance tests. A spec of its own is the definition of done the project uses for an area (spec README). delivery.md is already over 2,300 lines and is edited by every CI change. A separate test list keeps the corpus's tests from interleaving with the workflows'. naming.md is the naming algorithm. Its changes go to review at a gate. A test plan inside it would blur what needs that review. (ii) is the closest second: the examples runner is there. delivery.md keeps the CI side and points to corpus.md.
2. *Where the corpus lives.* (i) `corpus/` at the repository root; (ii) folders under `examples/`; (iii) test data inside `crates/partsmith-cli/tests/`. The rule takes (i), the safest. Technical design section 17 lists "the model corpus" beside the workspace, the UI and the docs in the one repository. The examples are documentation, one model per feature and option, each with a Python twin. Mixing whole parts into them would weaken runner tests 3 and 6 to 9 and would ask for twins the corpus does not need. (iii) hides a project-wide asset inside one crate, beside `tests/corpus/`, the reference stability documents of cli.md's tests, under a confusable name. The cost of (i) is one line in AGENTS.md's repository map.
3. *Where the checks run.* (i) Submodules of the examples runner in the CLI's test binary, run by `cargo test --workspace` in `rust` and by `rust-platforms`' `examples::` step; (ii) a job of its own in `ci.yml`; (iii) the nightly workflow. The rule takes (i), the safest. The checks run on every pull request and every push to `main`, on Linux, Windows and macOS, with no workflow change. They reuse the runner's comparison, bless, refusal under `CI` and parallel runs. The corpus and the examples then follow one set of rules. It is also the industry standard: a regression corpus that gates every merge. (ii) adds a job, its caches and its `deploy.needs` entry, and builds the workspace again. (iii) lets a regression merge and shows it a night later. If the checks outgrow their 3-minute budget, they move to a job of their own with `--release` by a new ADR (corpus.md section 5).
4. *How the oracle names a reference.* (i) The feature's id, then the reference's JSON Pointer within the feature record; (ii) a JSON Pointer into the whole document (`/features/3/edges/0`); (iii) the reference's stored query. The rule takes (i), the safest. A feature's id does not change when a script adds or moves features before it. (ii)'s index does. (iii) cannot tell apart two entries that store the same query. It also makes the oracle read like the thing it judges. The pointer within the record is the `field` that `list_uses` and feature messages already use.
5. *What counts as downstream.* (i) The references of the features whose prior state the script may change, by a rule the runner computes from the base and edited documents and the registry; (ii) every reference of the edited document; (iii) the references the author lists. The rule takes (i), the safest. Under (ii), references the edit cannot reach count as matches and inflate the metric toward its target. Under (iii), a hard reference can be left out and nothing notices. The rule leaves out a feature's own references when only that feature changed. That feature reads an unchanged state. It compares records without their labels. A label never changes geometry. It follows parameters through `Registry::dependencies` and `Expr::references`, both public, with no text scanning that would match role names such as `top`.
6. *Which references are scored.* (i) Element references, the objects with a `q` member; (ii) element references and the region references of extrude and revolve profiles. The rule takes (i), the safest that can be built from the protocol today. The metric measures the naming algorithm of naming.md, which resolves element references. Regions are matched by sketch.md's rules. No engine command resolves a stored region. Adding one is a protocol schema change for review at a gate. The reference stability corpus already covers regions (R01 to R07, R50, R66). corpus.md's open issue 1 keeps (ii) for a later spec.
7. *How a reference is resolved.* (i) Through the engine's commands in a headless session: `set_focus`, `resolve_references` and `list_elements`; (ii) a new field of recompute's reports with each reference's `Resolution`; (iii) the runner calls partsmith-naming against recompute's body states. The rule takes (i), the safest. It uses the path every host uses, from the document alone, with no change to a crate's public API. (ii) changes recompute's API for a test. (iii) duplicates the engine's choice of state. The cost of (i): the protocol's `Resolution` has no match method. A guess is then scored by its elements (corpus.md open issue 2).
8. *The match rule.* (i) An element name matches exactly that element, a list matches exactly those pieces in any order, and `"lost"` matches `missing` or `ambiguous`; (ii) a separate `"ambiguous"` expectation. All options are equally open. The rule takes (i), the safest: the plan's two forms, an element name and "lost", with a list for the pieces of a split. naming.md section 3.9 step 2 returns those pieces as one resolution. Features report an ambiguous reference as lost, with candidates (features.md section 3.8). The user sees the same thing. An oracle that guesses which kind of loss the resolver reports would test the resolver's internals, not the user's outcome.
9. *The target and the drift.* (i) Every run fails on drift from the stored outcomes until a bless, prints the metric with its mismatches, and the target is judged at the M2 gate; (ii) every run also fails when the metric is at or below 99 percent, from wave 1; (iii) nothing fails, and the metric is a report only. The rule takes (i), the safest for the oracle's honesty. Under (ii), one hard model that today's rules cannot meet would stop every merge of the project. That pressure would push test authors toward oracles the code already passes. The corpus would flatter the rules, and the metric would mean less. Drift already makes every change to an outcome fail until a reviewer sees it. (iii) lets a naming change move outcomes silently. (i) is also what the plan says: baselines that only track drift, every rule measured as it lands, and the target held in wave 5.
10. *Where the outcomes are stored.* (i) One `<stem>.outcomes.json` next to each edits file; (ii) one file for the whole corpus; (iii) no stored outcomes. The rule takes (i), the safest. A changed outcome shows in the pull request next to its model. Tracks that add models in parallel never edit the same file. (ii) makes every track touch one file. (iii) leaves drift untracked, against the plan.
11. *What the rebuild baseline holds.* (i) Feature statuses and each body's volume, area, bounding box and face count; (ii) the whole `rebuild --checks --json` report, as the examples store; (iii) the four measures alone. All three are equally open. (iii) is the least safe: it misses a fillet that fails with its body unchanged. (i) and (ii) are equally safe for what the corpus measures: the examples already hold the rest of the report. Neither is an industry standard. The rule takes (i), this work's recommendation. It holds the four measures that technical design section 16 and the plan name, and each feature's status. The status catches a feature that silently stops building. Under (ii), every change to a message's words or a print check would bless the corpus again.
12. *Which models carry scripts.* (i) Every example outside the benchmark that stores a reference, every corpus model and the four success parts; (ii) the corpus alone; (iii) a sample of examples. The rule takes (i), the plan's reading: the metric runs over the examples, the four parts and the corpus. A rule that covers every example that stores a reference is checkable and leaves none out. The benchmark is a performance document, not documentation (ADR 0051 decision 4).
13. *How many scripts.* (i) At least three per corpus model and success part, one an update and one an insertion, and at least one per example; (ii) one per model. Both are equally open. The rule takes (i), the safer: technical design section 16 names three kinds of scripted edits. One script per model would measure one kind. An update and an insertion are possible on every model. A move and a removal are not always valid. They are recommended, not checked.
14. *Which edits a script may hold.* (i) `update_parameter`, `add_parameter`, `add_body`, `add_feature`, `update_feature`, `remove_feature` and `move_feature`; (ii) any edit. The rule takes (i), the safest. These are the edits technical design section 16 names (a dimension through `update_feature` or `update_parameter`, a feature added upstream, a reorder), a removal or suppression, and the two that only make room for them (`add_parameter`, `add_body`). The downstream rule is exact for each. A change of units, printer or rollback needs rules of its own and is left for later (corpus.md 1.2).
15. *Coverage.* (i) Every registered feature type has a corpus model, and every type whose params can hold a reference has a reference scored in a corpus script, both read from the engine as soon as the code has them; (ii) only the table's counts, checked at the gate. The rule takes (i), the safest: a feature that lands without corpus models fails its own pull request. That is how "every M2 naming rule is measured as it lands" is held. It follows ADR 0051's coverage checks. (ii) finds a missing rule only at the gate.
16. *The edited text.* (i) Check 8 opens each edited document anew in a new session, with no cached result, checks that its save is byte-identical, and checks that the edit left every record it does not name as the base model has it; (ii) gate 4's check left to a test of its own elsewhere. Both are equally open. The rule takes (i), the safest: it holds gate 4 on every merge for every scripted edit of the examples and the corpus, which gate 4 names. A save after a rebuild alone cannot see a value that the first recompute already wrote into the edited text. The comparison with the base model sees it. `apply_edits` changes only the records its edits name. The runner already opens every edited document from its text alone. The two comparisons cost one save and one JSON comparison.
17. *Twins of the corpus models.* (i) None in M2; (ii) Python twins as the examples have. The rule takes (i), less code. Technical design section 17's twins are build123d scripts built by CI. They need a build123d environment and come with M4's release hardening (m2-plan.md section 5.2). Python twins of the partsmith API would test the round trip again. The examples already do that.
18. *An edits file with a problem of check 7, in check 8 (2026-11-08, found while building the track).* corpus.md section 2.7 leaves an edits file that cannot be read or is not JSON out of the other checks. It does not say what check 8 does with a file that is JSON but breaks a rule of section 2.4. The options: (i) check 8 runs none of its scripts and neither compares nor writes its outcomes, as for incomplete outcomes (section 3.4); (ii) check 8 runs the scripts it can read; (iii) check 8 reports the file's shape problems again. The rule takes (i), the safest. No outcome is stored for an oracle that check 7 refuses, no problem is reported twice, and check 7 fails the run until the file is fixed.
19. *An engine command that fails where section 3.2 expects none (2026-11-08, found while building the track).* Section 4 names the failures of steps 1, 3 and 5. The other commands of section 3.2 and the save of section 3.5 fail only on a fault of the engine. The options: (i) the problem `{file}: script {name}: {command} failed: {message}`, after which the script stops and its file's outcomes are incomplete; (ii) a panic, with the test binary ended before the other problems are collected; (iii) the failure left out. The rule takes (i), the safest. Each check collects every problem before it fails (section 2.7), and the problem names the command and the engine's message.

## Consequences

- [corpus.md](../spec/corpus.md): the whole spec, with tests 1 to 53 and its open issues.
- delivery.md: the scope line and its ADR list, 1.2's corpus row, 1.3, 2.1's files, 2.8's rule, its runner tests 1 and 10 and its bless and adding notes, 3.7, section 5's row of the corpus checks, test 155, and open issue 4.
- naming.md: a pointer in section 1 and a status line on open issue 6 (the longest name).
- The test author writes, in wave 1, the edits files of the 97 examples that store a reference and the 46 wave-1 models with theirs. The implementer adds `crates/partsmith-cli/tests/examples/model_corpus.rs` and `crates/partsmith-cli/tests/examples/naming_metric.rs`, declares them in `examples.rs`, adds `partsmith-naming` as a development dependency of `partsmith-cli`, and blesses the expected results and outcomes. No workflow, schema or product code changes.
- Each track of waves 2 to 4 adds its row's models with their scripts. A cut ADR of the plan's section 6 item 11 replaces its feature's models in the table.
- Every merge rebuilds the corpus and runs every script, about three rebuilds each. The estimate is 3 minutes in `rust`, measured by the first CI run.
- Handoffs to files outside this work: AGENTS.md's repository map gains `corpus/`; the CLI's AGENTS.md names the two modules; architecture.md's "Testing" names the corpus and the metric; the spec README's index gains corpus.md; the ADR log gains this record.
- Not covered, and recorded in corpus.md's open issues: region references, guesses scored by their elements, the measured time, a crash inside OCCT during check 8, the corpus as fuzzing seeds, and the first measurement of the metric.
