# 0075: Loft rails and end conditions wait for a later milestone

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05); needs human review at the M2 gate: it sets aside a criterion of kernel.md section 8.4 after the spike ran (decision 5). The loft spike did not confirm the M2 plan's choice 18 (kernel.md section 8.4). [ADR 0071](0071-kernel-spikes-and-facade-calls-that-poll-an-interrupt.md) decision 3 then asks for a new ADR before features/loft.md. This ADR replaces choice 18 for rails and settles what an end condition promises. M2's loft (item 30) keeps lofts between profiles, point ends and a centerline. Rails and tangent or curvature end conditions move to M3, and features/loft.md lists them as later. It changes no file format, protocol schema, naming algorithm, license or dependency.

## Context

- **Choice 18.** Option (a): `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` for lofts between profiles with point ends, and `BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell` with several sections along a spine for rails and a centerline. Option (b): surfaces filled by hand with `GeomFill`. The plan picked (a), "confirmed by wave 1's spike for rails and end conditions, the hard cases". It turned (b) down as hand-built surfaces.
- **Section 8.4.** A rail lies on its lateral face within 1e-6 mm. For an end condition, the angle between the loft's face and the neighbor face along the end edge lies within 1e-6 rad of the one asked. End conditions pass when one construction meets them on every input that has one. End conditions that no construction meets are listed in features/loft.md as later, by a new ADR. The Decision names no case for rails that fail.
- **Rails.** The input runs a cubic Bezier rail from a corner of a 40 mm square to a corner of a 20 mm square 40 mm above it. It bulges up to 7.14 mm from the straight line between the corners. The pipe shell ran in all six `SetMode` modes. `free` and `free curvilinear` built valid lofts whose faces lie 7.133 and 6.824 mm from the rail: the rail only orients the sections. `contact` and `contact curvilinear` were not done after 1.16 and 1.50 s. `border` raised `Standard_ConstructionError: gp_Trsf::SetScaleFactor`, and `border curvilinear` was not done after 0.43 s. In OCCT 7.9.3's source, `ContactOnBorder` keeps only the last section added, and its plane trihedron never sets the point on the guide that its scaling law reads. No construction section 8.4 names follows the rail.
- **Without rails.** `ThruSections`, ruled and smooth, passed a square to a circle to a turned square, a square to a point and 4 edges to 3. The pipe shell passed the same along a straight centerline, and two sections along a quarter-circle centerline. Every section lies on its loft within 8.9e-9 mm, parity holds, and the three-section loft takes 6.70 to 8.19 ms natively against 100 ms.
- **End conditions.** The spike asked 0 rad against a prism of the loft's end face, on three straight lofts between profiles, tangent and curvature. The ruled loft with one or two extra sections, offset 1, 0.1 or 0.01 mm, meets 0 rad on all six inputs. Its first faces are the planar walls of a prism of the end section. The plain loft's crease of 0.13 to 0.48 rad moves to the extra section. Under section 8.4's end-condition check as written, end conditions therefore passed, and the Decision's second sentence did not apply. The smooth loft and the pipe shell with extra sections meet the tangent angle on no input. With two extra sections 0.01 mm apart they meet the curvature angle on some inputs only, and the smooth `three-sections` loft then differs across targets (5.0e-6 relative in volume). `BRepOffsetAPI_MakeFilling` on the smooth loft meets no condition (0.245 to 0.464 rad with G1), and its G2 constraint throws in OCCT 7.9.3. No end condition ran on a curved centerline, where only the pipe shell builds.

## Decision

1. **Lofts without rails take choice 18's (a).** features/loft.md (wave 3, item 30) specifies lofts between profiles with `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` (solid, ruled or smooth, `CheckCompatibility(true)`, `AddVertex` for a point end). It specifies lofts along a centerline with `BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell` (each section added, `SetTolerance(1e-6, 1e-6, 1e-6)`, `MakeSolid`).
2. **Rails move to M3.** features/loft.md lists guide rails as later. Choice 18's pipe shell with a rail through `SetMode` is not specified.
3. **An end condition is a property of the loft's own surface.** A tangent condition makes the loft's lateral surface tangent to the neighbor face across the end edge. A curvature condition makes it curvature continuous there. In both, the lateral surface has no crease between the end section and the next profile the user gave. An extra section that a construction adds is not a profile. The angle at the end edge alone does not meet either condition.
4. **End conditions move to M3.** No construction tried meets item 3's reading on every input. The ruled loft with extra sections meets the angle at the end edge, but it keeps a crease of 0.13 to 0.48 rad, 0.01 to 1 mm inside the loft. The smooth loft and the pipe shell with extra sections miss the tangent angle on every input. features/loft.md lists tangent and curvature end conditions as later.
5. **A criterion fixed before the run is set aside.** Under section 8.4's end-condition check as written, the ruled loft with extra sections met both end conditions on all six inputs. Item 3 replaces that check's reading after the result. ADR 0071's alternative 2 fixes criteria before a spike runs, because a criterion written first cannot bend to a result. Item 3 reads the check more strictly than its words, and its outcome ships less: it confirms no construction, and end conditions wait for M3. A stricter reading cannot let a failing construction pass, which is what alternative 2 guards against. It still changes a criterion after its result, and the owner reviews it at the M2 gate.
6. **The cut follows the plan's rule.** Rails and both end conditions move to M3 by this ADR, in the form of m2-plan.md section 6 item 11, never by weakening a test. That rule cuts work after an overrun at the checkpoint after wave 2. Here the cause is the spike's result. The plan names no other way to move part of an item to a later milestone, and all of loft heads its cut order. They are exceptions of gate 3's loft options, which leave out what a cut ADR moves to M3. Gate 2 covers technical design section 7, the sketcher, which holds no loft option, and its list gains nothing.

## Alternatives considered

Every option is open source, built on OCCT. The first criterion of the rule picks none of them. The rule then takes the safest, then the industry standard, then the recommendation of this work. Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *Rails.* Options: (i) listed as later; (ii) choice 18's option (b), the lateral faces filled by hand with `GeomFill`; (iii) the pipe shell with a scaling law computed from the rail (`SetLaw`), which section 8.4 does not name. The rule takes (i), the safest. No construction measured builds a loft on its rail. The plan turned (b) down as hand-built surfaces. (iii) has no measurement and no pass criteria, and ADR 0071's alternative 2 fixes the criteria before a construction runs.
2. *What an end condition promises.* Options: (i) the angle along the end edge, as section 8.4's check measures it; (ii) the loft's lateral surface tangent or curvature continuous with the neighbor face, with no crease before the next profile. The rule takes (ii), the safest. Under (i) a loft asked for a tangent end keeps the plain loft's crease of up to 0.48 rad, moved 0.01 mm into the loft: a promise the geometry does not keep. The plan's choice 17 takes the same stance: never a silent distortion.
3. *End conditions under (ii).* Options: (i) listed as later, as section 8.4's Decision says for conditions no construction meets; (ii) the smooth loft with two extra sections 0.01 mm apart; (iii) `MakeFilling` on the loft's end faces. The rule takes (i), the safest. (ii) misses the tangent angle on every input and the curvature angle on `point-end`, and its results differ across targets on `three-sections`. (iii) meets no input, moves the loft off the middle section it spans (1.110 mm on `three-sections`) and throws for G2.

## Consequences

- features/loft.md lists rails and both end conditions as later, citing this ADR. m2-plan.md item 30, section 3.3 and section 5.1 say they move to M3.
- M3's loft work measures rails and end conditions again, with pass criteria fixed first: for rails, option (iii) above or choice 18's (b); for end conditions, a construction whose surface meets item 3's reading, on curved centerlines too.
- `ThruSections::Generated` of a section edge is by position, not by segment, and a ruled loft's middle-section edges and vertices have no name (kernel.md section 8.4). features/loft.md names a lateral face with its segment from the loft's order (kernel.md open issue 21).
- The OCCT 7.9.3 defects the spike found in `BRepFill_Filling` and in the pipe shell's `ContactOnBorder` mode are kernel.md open issue 22. The sweep's rail modes of wave 4 use the same pipe shell modes (kernel.md open issue 21 (g)).
- The spike's branch adds 778,744 bytes to the web core, `MakeFilling` among them. A loft without the filling links less, by an amount not measured.
- kernel.md section 8.4's Results and ADR 0071's note of 2026-11-16 point here.
