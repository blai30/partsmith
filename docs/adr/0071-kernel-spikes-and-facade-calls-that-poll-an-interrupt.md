# 0071: Kernel spikes on throwaway branches, and facade calls that poll an interrupt

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the choices below. It fixes how the M2 plan's item 10 runs its kernel spikes and how partsmith-occt-sys carries the interrupt of [ADR 0063](0063-cancel-through-a-host-flag-outside-the-session.md) into OCCT. No file format, protocol schema, naming, license or dependency change: every OCCT toolkit the spikes try is already linked (kernel.md section 3.12 item 3), and the interrupt adds no export, no `unsafe` item and no crate. The facade's public API changes: the calls that poll take a trailing `&Interrupt`, `KernelErrorKind` gains `Canceled` and `Operation` gains `WaitForInterrupt`, public API changes like those of ADR 0045 and ADR 0053. The spike results that confirm or replace the plan's choices 15, 18 and 19 are recorded in kernel.md section 8 and in dated notes here, or in a new ADR where a result replaces a choice.

## Context

- **Spikes before specs.** The M2 plan's item 10 measures the riskiest kernel constructions in wave 1, while the feature specs of waves 2 to 4 are still unwritten: an exact helix and a modeled thread on a cap, wrap emboss on a cylinder and a cone, a loft with rails, up-to-face and tapered prisms, the rib construction, and edge projection, section by the sketch plane and silhouette. Gate 18 asks for the measurements in kernel.md, the linked core's size among them. The plan says no product code merges from a spike. Choice 15 asks for an ADR with the thread spike's result. Choices 18 and 19 are confirmed or replaced by their spikes.
- **The web core's room.** The linked core was 19,710,890 bytes (18.8 MiB) on 2026-10-23 against Cloudflare Pages' 25 MiB per-file limit (delivery.md open issue 2, m2-plan.md risk 8). Static linking keeps only the OCCT code a call reaches. A spike that reaches hidden-line removal (`TKHLR`) or the offset and filling algorithms grows the core even though their toolkits are already in the link list.
- **The kernel has no progress hook at a3f4573.** No facade call passes a `Message_ProgressRange` to OCCT. ADR 0063 settled the route: OCCT's progress indicator polled against a flag the host holds, an Emscripten JavaScript import on the web, no fourth C ABI export, `unsafe` only in partsmith-occt-sys. It left the facade's form to kernel.md.
- **What OCCT 7.9.3 polls.** A probe program linked against the native OCCT install, with a `Message_ProgressIndicator` that counts `UserBreak` calls, measured on 2026-11-07 (release libraries, 4-core development container): `BRepAlgoAPI_Fuse` of two boxes 650 polls; `BRepAlgoAPI_Cut` of 194 cylinders from a 200 mm plate 29,290 polls, the longest gap 20.5 ms of 440 ms; `BRepAlgoAPI_Splitter` 408; `BRepOffsetAPI_MakeThickSolid` 180; `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` with OCCT's default mesher, of a sphere of radius 250 at 0.0125 mm, 100,473, the longest gap 583 ms, inside its one face; `ShapeFix_Shape` 43; the STEP writer's and reader's `Transfer` 16 and 60 for a box. `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet::Build` takes a range and never polls (`ChFi3d_Builder` declares none), and neither does `BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism`. `BRepCheck_Analyzer`, `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` and the STEP reader's `ReadStream` take no range. On a user break, the booleans and the splitter end with `BOPAlgo_AlertUserBreak`, the mesher and the STEP writer report not done, and the thick solid stopped at its sixth poll threw `Standard_NoSuchObject: NCollection_DataMap::Find`.
- **Edge and vertex history.** naming.md section 3.12 carries direct-generator names through the `modified` lists of edges and vertices ([ADR 0060](0060-direct-generator-names-for-edges-and-vertices.md)). At a3f4573 no kernel test pinned the edge and vertex lists of booleans, splits and shells, or of the edges a fillet keeps. Tests 44 and 54 pinned only the rounded edge and a transform's images. A scratch run of the facade on 2026-11-07 found the fuse, cut, fillet, split and shell cases of kernel.md tests 146 to 150 met with OCCT's history as the shim merges it.

## Decision

1. **Spikes run on throwaway branches** (kernel.md section 8.1). Each spike is a branch `spike/kernel-<name>` from the wave 1 branch, never merged and kept until the M2 gate. It adds the construction to partsmith-occt-sys there, with tests in its `tests/` directory, and calls it once from the web core's `main` so the linker keeps its code. Its results are recorded in kernel.md section 8 with the branch's commit. A wave 2 to 4 feature spec then specifies the construction it picked, and its code is written and tested anew from that spec.
2. **One protocol for every spike.** Every input is measured for validity as built and after repair, a closed mesh, the spike's geometry checks, time (release, median of 5, natively and under Node), native and WebAssembly parity by ADR 0042's comparison (counts exact, volume, area and bounding box within 1e-9 relative), the core's size delta from the branch's base, and whether the construction's OCCT algorithms poll. A construction passes when every input passes every criterion of its subsection. For the thread on the cap and the 20-character label, the longest gap between two polls is at most 150 ms natively (alternative 11).
3. **How a result becomes a decision.** A spike that confirms the plan's choice adds a dated note to this ADR. One that replaces it needs a new ADR settled under the owner's rule before its feature spec. Choice 15 gets the note or the ADR either way, as the plan asks.
4. **The interrupt is read by the shim alone** (kernel.md section 3.13). `Interrupt` is a plain Rust value: a shared `AtomicU64` word and the request id it waits for, and on the web core the page's word too. The Rust facade never reads it. Its argument checks run first. The shim gives each polling call one `Message_ProgressIndicator` whose `UserBreak` calls `Interrupt::is_requested` through the bridge's `extern "Rust"` block, and latches once it answers true. The shim also polls before its first OCCT algorithm and between its own steps.
5. **A stop is decided by the indicator, not by the algorithm.** When the indicator answered true at least once, the call fails with `KernelErrorKind::Canceled` and no shape, however the algorithm ended: done, not done, an alert, a status or an exception.
6. **The calls that poll take a trailing `interrupt: &Interrupt`**: `extrude`, `revolve`, `fuse`, `cut`, `common`, `split`, `fillet`, `chamfer`, `shell`, `Shape::tessellate`, `write_step` and `read_step`. A caller that never stops passes `&Interrupt::new()`.
7. **The web import goes through the existing bridge.** The shim defines `partsmith_cancel_word` with `EM_JS`, and a C++ function `page_cancel_word` returns its value. The bridge declares `page_cancel_word` in its existing `unsafe extern "C++"` block, on `target_os = "emscripten"` only. Rust compares the value with the request id.
8. **`wait_for_interrupt` is public.** It is the polling loop the callers' tests of features.md test 45 and recompute.md test 78 run, with its own `Operation::WaitForInterrupt`.
9. **Texts.** `Canceled` has the hint `It was stopped before it finished.` and the user message `Partsmith stopped this step before it finished.` for every operation. Features and the engine word a stop themselves (features.md section 4.1, engine.md section 3.26).
10. **Edge and vertex history** follows kernel.md section 3.3 item 7, pinned by tests 146 to 150, with no augmentation needed today. Later operations report their generators from OCCT's history (section 3.3 item 8).

## Alternatives considered

Every option is open source, built on OCCT and code in this repository. The first criterion of the rule picks none of them. The rule then takes the safest, then the industry standard, then the recommendation of this work. Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *Where spike code lives.* Options: (i) a throwaway branch per spike, never merged, its results in kernel.md with the commit; (ii) the spike merged behind a cargo feature; (iii) a scratch crate outside the workspace that links OCCT directly. The rule takes (i), the safest: `main` stays production-grade with no temporary code (AGENTS.md principle 1), and the plan's choice 25 took the same form for the Pyodide spike. Under (ii) code that no spec covers would sit on `main`. Under (iii) the web core's size and the WebAssembly parity of the real build could not be measured.
2. *Pass criteria.* Options: (i) fixed per spike in kernel.md before the spike runs; (ii) judged after the measurements. The rule takes (i), the safest: a criterion written first cannot bend to a result.
3. *Who reads the interrupt.* Options: (i) the shim's indicator only, with polls of its own between steps; (ii) also the Rust facade, before and after each shim call; (iii) only OCCT's own polls. The rule takes (i), the safest. One reader gives one rule for every call: argument checks first, then the shim. Under (ii) two readers could disagree when the word changes between them. Under (iii) a fillet, which OCCT 7.9.3 never polls inside, could not stop even when it was requested before it started.
4. *Deciding a stop.* Options: (i) the indicator's record; (ii) each algorithm's own status (`BOPAlgo_AlertUserBreak`, `BRepOffset_UserBreak`, not done). The rule takes (i), the safest: OCCT's algorithms report a break in different ways, and the thick solid threw an unrelated exception after one. Under (ii) a stop could surface as a kernel failure with a misleading hint.
5. *The facade's form.* Options: (i) a trailing `&Interrupt` on every call that polls; (ii) a second function per call (`fuse_interruptible`); (iii) an interrupt in thread-local state. The rule takes (i). It is the safest: every call site states whether it can stop, and nothing reads hidden state, which (iii) would add against the invariant of no global mutable state in the core. It is also the industry standard: OCCT's own algorithms take their `Message_ProgressRange` as a trailing parameter. Under (ii) the API doubles (AGENTS.md principle 5).
6. *How the web import is called.* Options: (i) a C++ function in the shim that calls the `EM_JS` import, declared in the existing `unsafe extern "C++"` block; (ii) a new `unsafe extern "C"` block in Rust that declares the import and one `unsafe` call of it, the form the cancel step's handoff proposed. The rule takes (i), the safest: cxx checks the declaration against the C++ definition at build time, which a raw `extern "C"` declaration does not, and kernel.md section 3.9 item 8's rule that the only `unsafe` code is the bridge block and the `Send` and `Sync` impls of `Shape` stays true. Both keep the import, its reader and its call in partsmith-occt-sys (ADR 0063 alternative 7, ADR 0010's note of 2026-11-07).
7. *`wait_for_interrupt` in the API.* Options: (i) public in every build; (ii) behind a cargo feature that only dev-dependencies enable. The rule takes (i), the safest: the build the tests run is the build the product ships, with no feature that only tests switch on. The call only waits and stops. It writes nothing and holds no lock.
8. *What a request id of 0 means.* Options: (i) never requested, whatever the word holds; (ii) requested while the word holds 0. The rule takes (i), the safest: the word starts at 0 and holds 0 after the page clears it, and engine.md section 3.26 says a request with id 0 never stops. Under (ii) every interrupt for request 0 would read as requested.
9. *Latching.* Options: (i) the indicator keeps answering true for the rest of the call once it answered true; (ii) it asks again at each poll. The rule takes (i), the safest: an OCCT algorithm that saw a break must not see the work resume, and the word can change while the call runs.
10. *Which calls take an interrupt.* Options: (i) the calls that run long OCCT algorithms on the bodies M1 builds: `extrude`, `revolve`, the booleans, `split`, `fillet`, `chamfer`, `shell`, `tessellate` and the STEP calls, four of which stop only at the shim's polls between their steps; (ii) also every other call that builds or checks a shape, `transform`, `section`, `make_profile`, `compound` and `is_valid` among them; (iii) only the calls OCCT polls inside. The rule takes (i), the safest. Under (iii) a fillet requested before it starts would still run its blend and its check, the longest unpolled steps of M1's features. Under (ii) more public signatures change for little gain. Each added call is one OCCT step, or for a mirror or a scale a copy and its check. A stop could skip at most that check, which takes about 128 ms on `PLATE` (kernel.md section 3.5), within gate 11's 200 ms on the desktop. The next polling call of the build stops at its first poll. `section` has no caller in a build at a3f4573. Item 26's kernel spec gives it an interrupt if the section by the sketch plane needs one.
11. *A spike criterion on poll gaps.* Options: (i) the spikes only measure the polls and the longest gap; (ii) the thread on the cap and the 20-character label, which gate 11 times, also pass only when their longest gap is at most 150 ms natively, or with a new ADR that records the gap. The rule takes (ii), the safest: under (i) a construction whose loft or thickening runs seconds without a poll would pass its spike and break gate 11 only in wave 4. 150 ms leaves 50 ms of the desktop's 200 ms for the rest of the response, and three times both, 450 ms and 150 ms, fit the web's 600 ms.

## Consequences

- kernel.md gains section 3.13 (Interrupts), section 3.3 items 7 and 8, the contract of item 14's profile curves in section 2.5, section 8 (the spikes), tests 146 to 168, budget rows and open issue 17.
- partsmith-occt-sys gains `Interrupt`, `wait_for_interrupt`, `cpp/interrupt.cpp`, `Partsmith_Canceled`, the `extern "Rust"` block and, on the web core, `page_cancel_word` with its `EM_JS` import. Its test binaries for `wasm32-unknown-emscripten` link a pre-js file that sets the page's word to 7 (kernel.md section 3.12 item 9).
- Every caller of a polling call passes an interrupt: partsmith-features its `BuildContext::interrupt()` (features.md section 2.6), partsmith-io's STEP calls and the engine's export the request's interrupt, and the engine's display tessellation `Interrupt::new()` (engine.md section 3.26).
- Some calls stop only between their steps: OCCT 7.9.3 never polls inside the blend builder, the validity check, unification, STEP parsing or writing, or some phases of a face's triangulation (kernel.md open issue 17). Their cancel latency is that of their longest step. kernel.md section 5 therefore budgets the cancel latency of the shell of `RC` alone, and records the others in open issue 17 once measured.
- The spikes run after this round, in wave 1. Their branches stay until the gate. Their results reach the feature specs through kernel.md section 8 and this ADR's notes.
