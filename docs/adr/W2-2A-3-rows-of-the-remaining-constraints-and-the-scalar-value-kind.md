# W2-2A-3: Rows of the remaining constraints, and the scalar value kind

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the M2 plan's open choices "Distances to circles and between lines, arc length scope, smooth and symmetric pairing" and "Snell's law, and unitless values", decided as the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 accept them ([m2-wave2-decisions.md](../design/m2-wave2-decisions.md) section 3). It changes the protocol schema: `ValueKind` gains `scalar`, and the description of `ConstraintState::value` covers plain numbers (protocol.md section 2.6). That goes to human review at the M2 gate with the protocol additions (m2-plan.md gate 19). The rows change no file format beyond [ADR W2-2A-1](W2-2A-1-constraint-records-of-the-remaining-sketch-constraints.md)'s records. It extends [ADR 0077](0077-solver-rows-of-conics-and-splines.md)'s rows of conics and splines, and builds on [ADR 0058](0058-exact-trust-region-step.md)'s exact step.

## Context

- **Rows in mm.** Every residual is a length in mm with analytic derivatives (sketch.md section 3.6.5). The exact step, the diagnosis and the drag rely on it. Angular rows reach mm through the warm-start scale `S` (section 3.6.3).
- **Warm-start choices.** M1 fixes a side or a mode once from the warm start where a relation has several branches: the side of a tangent line or of a point-line distance, and the external or internal mode of two tangent circles (section 3.6.3).
- **Parity and budgets.** Native and WebAssembly results agree within 1e-9 (section 3.12). The drag median is 4 ms (m2-plan.md gate 9). Quadrature on splines would iterate to a tolerance in every row and every frame (m2-plan.md risk 6).
- **Values.** `ValueKind` has `Length` and `Angle` (`crates/partsmith-sketch/src/params.rs`). A weight and a ratio of refractive indices have no unit. partsmith-model's `FieldUnit::Scalar` types a plain number (`crates/partsmith-model/src/manifest.rs`), displayed by `Quantity::plain` (features.md section 3.12).

## Decision

1. **Smooth (G2).** At the junction where an end of one curve meets an end of the other through `coincident` constraints, two rows: the touch-point tangent row of M1 and item 14, `S * cross(t1, t2)`, and a curvature row, `S^2 * cross(t1, k1 - k2)`, with `k1` and `k2` the curvature vectors of the two curves at the junction. A curvature vector does not depend on the direction a curve runs, so the rows hold whichever ends meet. At least one curve is a conic or a spline: a line and an arc, or two arcs, would always conflict. Two curves with no junction fail validation with `NoJunction`.
2. **Arc length on circular arcs only**, `rad(K) * sweep - v`, with the sweep measured from its warm-start value through `atan2` of the change, so it never jumps at 0 or at pi. A converged solve whose sweep leaves `(0, 2 * pi)` counts as not converged: the length cannot be reached at that radius.
3. **Circle distances take a side or a mode at the warm start**, as `tangent` does: a point outside or inside the circle, and two circles apart or one inside the other, by the same nearer-relation rule as M1's tangent mode.
4. **Line-line distance stays M1's point-line distance** for parallel lines, and an angle for the others ([ADR W2-2A-4](W2-2A-4-block-angle-to-an-axis-and-line-distances-need-no-new-type.md)).
5. **Symmetric curves.** Two lines pair their ends at the warm start: start with start and end with end, unless the crossed pairing is nearer to the mirror images (start with start on a tie). Two circles have symmetric centers and equal radii. Two arcs pair the start of the first with the end of the second, since a mirror image runs the other way round: symmetric centers, symmetric start and end, and one row that turns the second's start onto the mirror of the first's end, as M1's `fix` of an arc holds its end.
6. **Arc midpoint in half-angle form**: `p - c - rad(K) * unit(perp(s - e))`, two rows with no trigonometry and one solution for every sweep but 0. The perpendicular of the chord, turned a quarter turn counterclockwise, points at the midpoint of the counterclockwise arc from `s` to `e` whatever its sweep.
7. **Refraction in FreeCAD's form, without trigonometry.** With `r1` the direction of the incoming ray into its end, `r2` that of the outgoing ray out of its start and `tau` the boundary's unit tangent at the incoming end, one row: `S * (dot(tau, r1) - v * dot(tau, r2))`. `dot(tau, r)` is the sine of the angle between the ray and the boundary's normal. The coincidence of the two ends and the point on the boundary are the user's constraints, which the UI adds with it (track 3B). The boundary is a line, a circle, an arc or an axis in M2.
8. **`ValueKind::Scalar`** for `refraction` and `weight`: the caller evaluates the expression as a plain number (features/sketch.md section 3.2 gives the unit `Scalar`), messages write it without a unit, and the protocol's measured value is that number.

## Alternatives considered

Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the most open-source approach, then the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Every option is in-house Rust under Apache-2.0: the first criterion ties.

1. *Smooth's operands.* (a) A conic or spline with any curve that has ends; (b) any two curves, where a line and an arc always conflict. The rule takes (a), the safest: (b) accepts records that can never hold. Fusion's curvature (G2) constraint also asks for a spline. 
2. *Smooth's rows.* (a) The tangent row and the curvature row at a junction the user's coincident makes; (b) the curvature row alone, with the user's tangent beside it; (c) a hidden parameter on each curve, as a tangent without a touch point has. The rule takes (a), the safest: G2 includes G1, so (b) lets a smooth hold without a tangent, and (c) adds two variables and three rows for a relation that, in a sketch, always sits at a junction. The curvature row's scale `S^2` keeps it in mm. A tangent already on the same pair makes the smooth's tangent row dependent, and the smooth is reported redundant, as M1 reports a constraint added last.
3. *Arc length's scope.* (a) Circular arcs only; (b) every curve with ends, by an in-house quadrature. The rule takes (a), the safest: (b) iterates to a tolerance in every row of every frame and puts 1e-9 parity and the drag budget at risk (m2-plan.md risk 6). The length of a line is M1's `distance`. Arc lengths of elliptical arcs and splines go to M3 (m2-plan.md section 5.1, note of 2026-11-23).
4. *Circle distances.* (a) A side or mode fixed at the warm start; (b) a signed value. The rule takes (a), the safest and the M1 pattern: the geometry stays on the side it starts on, and a value is never negative.
5. *Symmetric pairing.* (a) Start with start; (b) start with end; (c) chosen at the warm start; (d) explicit point lists. The rule takes (c) for lines, the safest: (a) and (b) each turn one of the two drawings a user makes inside out. (d) is M1's `symmetric` on points, which the user still has. Arcs have no choice: a mirror image of a counterclockwise arc runs from the mirror of its end.
6. *Arc midpoint.* (a) The half-angle form of decision 6; (b) on the circle and equidistant from both ends; (c) the angle of the midpoint by `atan2`. The rule takes (a), the safest: (b) also holds at the point opposite the midpoint, and (c) wraps at 2 pi. (a) has one solution and smooth rows.
7. *Snell's law.* Shape: (a) FreeCAD's form, with the coincidence and the point on the boundary as separate constraints; (b) one constraint that also states them. Rows: (i) sines from dot products; (ii) angles by `atan2`. The rule takes (a) and (i), the industry standard and the safest: FreeCAD's form keeps each relation one constraint the user can delete, and (i) has no branch.
8. *Unitless values.* (a) `ValueKind::Scalar`, mapped to `FieldUnit::Scalar`; (b) plain numbers outside `expr`. The rule takes (a), the safest: a weight or a ratio then follows a parameter and is checked like every dimension.
