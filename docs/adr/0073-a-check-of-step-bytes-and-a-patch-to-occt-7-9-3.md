# 0073: A check of STEP bytes before OCCT reads them, and a patch to OCCT 7.9.3

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05); needs human review at the M2 gate (m2-plan.md gates 15 and 19). It fixes the six `step_read` findings of ADR 0067's note of 2026-11-15, the track's 60 s run and the track's review, delivery.md section 3.8 findings 2 to 7. Two of its parts change decisions the owner made in M1 and go to review: Partsmith now builds and ships OCCT 7.9.3 with a patch, which amends [ADR 0003](0003-pin-occt-and-build-from-source.md) (an unmodified pinned release) and [ADR 0034](0034-occt-linking-and-lgpl-obligations.md) (`build-occt.sh` applies no patches). Also for review: the license of the patch file, the two OCCT statements of the notices, the new contents and SHA-256 of the OCCT source archive, the OCCT cache keys that move, and what `read_step` now refuses. Nothing changes in the file format, the protocol schema, the naming algorithm or the dependencies.

## Context

It ends on a SEGV at address 0 in `Standard_Transient::IsKind`, from `Interface_InterfaceModel::AddEntity` under `Interface_FileReaderTool::LoadedEntity`, inside `ReadStream`. `130f8c7ee36ea7353f94107b5848ebdbfbcfe785` (15,303 bytes) is a `SURFACE_CURVE` whose curve is itself. `StepToGeom::MakeCurve` then calls itself until the stack overflows, inside `STEPCAFControl_Reader::Transfer`. `51283b77f54210609cf65b501f3c5734020dc09f` (15,292 bytes) is an `EDGE_CURVE` that ends at a plain `VERTEX`. It ends on a SEGV at address `0x18` in `StepShape_VertexPoint::VertexGeometry`, from `RWStepShape_RWEdgeCurve::Check`, inside `ReadStream`. The note also showed that a patch per recursion was not complete: a build with two such patches stopped on the third crash.
- **Two more findings.** The track's 60 s run after the first fixes found `1274c3a5727db947d6b2e2220687a8d6a3aa3029` (finding 6, 5,610 bytes): the import example's STEP file with two broken lines and the lines between them left out (delivery.md 3.8 gives its lines). OCCT's error recovery numbers the records that survive so that an oriented edge resolves to an oriented edge, and `StepShape_OrientedEdge::EdgeStart` follows that cycle until the stack overflows. The bytes hold no cycle. The track's review then found `step_read` stopping on libFuzzer timeouts inside `StepData_StepReaderData::SetEntityNumbers` (finding 7). A first record had put these timeouts down to the container pausing the fuzzer. The review showed the fuzzer running at full speed during the stall, and valgrind reporting reads of memory that function never set. Outside a scope, OCCT records a number defined twice as the fail `Ident defined SEVERAL TIMES` in the same pass.
  - The grammar of `src/StepFile/step.yacc` prints `***  Warning : Export List not yet processed` with `printf` for the export list of an `ENDSCOPE`, past OCCT's messenger. OCCT does not process the list.

## Decision

1. **A check of the bytes before OCCT** (kernel.md section 3.8.1 read items 6 and 7). `read_step` first runs `check_step_bytes` of `crates/partsmith-occt-sys/src/step_check.rs` on the bytes, before the shim and its mutex. It reads the bytes with the rules of OCCT's lexer and refuses, with kind `ReadFailed`, exception `Partsmith_ReadError` and the user message `The file is not a valid STEP file.`:
   - a parameter list that breaks STEP's syntax (read item 7);
   - an instance number larger than 2147483647 (read item 6);
   - a number defined more than once, and a scope (read item 6);
   - instance references that form a cycle (read item 6);
   - a chain of more than 500 references (read item 6).
2. **A patch to OCCT 7.9.3** (kernel.md read item 8) for the two faults no check of the bytes can refuse by class: the null read of finding 5 and the recovery-built cycle of oriented edges of finding 6. `scripts/occt-7.9.3.patch` makes `RWStepShape_RWOrientedEdge::ReadStep` give the fail `edge_element is an oriented_edge` for an oriented edge whose edge element is an oriented edge, and drops the edge element (ISO 10303-42 oriented_edge WR1). `StepShape_OrientedEdge::EdgeStart` and `EdgeEnd` then recurse at most one step. It makes `RWStepShape_RWEdgeCurve::ReadStep` ask for a `StepShape_VertexPoint` at both ends of an edge curve. A plain `VERTEX` then gives the load fail `Parameter n0.3 (edge_end) : Entity has illegal type` (`n0.2 (edge_start)` for the start). The check skips an edge curve that failed to load, and read item 4 refuses the file before the transfer. `scripts/build-occt.sh` applies the patch with `git apply` before the build, to its fresh clone of the tag or to an unchanged clone of the pinned commit. `scripts/occt-source-archive.sh` adds it to the OCCT source archive. Every OCCT cache key hashes it.

## Choices

Each choice is dated 2026-11-15 and Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *The class fix of the stack overflows.* (i) Refuse a file whose instance references form a cycle, before `ReadStream`; (ii) a patch per recursion; (iii) a depth counter patched into each recursion of OCCT's STEP code. All three are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest: one check bounds every recursion that a cycle drives, also those no input has reached yet. ADR 0067's note showed (ii) incomplete: a build with two such patches stopped on the third crash. (iii) needs a counter in every recursive function of the STEP reader, and their number is not known.
2. *A bound on chains without a cycle.* (i) Refuse a chain of more than 500 references; (ii) no bound; (iii) a bound just under the shallowest overflow measured, such as 2,500; (iv) a bound per entity type. All four are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest. A long chain overflows OCCT the way a cycle does: kernel.md test 187's 100,000 oriented edges overflowed the stack natively and under Node. 500 is more than 30 times the deepest chain of every file measured (15 references) and an eighth of the shallowest overflow measured, 4,000 trimmed curves under Node (the Note). (ii) leaves test 187 crashing. (iii) leaves no margin for a recursion with larger frames than the three measured, or for a browser's stack. (iv) mirrors OCCT's recursions in the check, as a patch per site would.
3. *What the check reads.* (i) The bytes, with the rules of OCCT's lexer; (ii) OCCT's model, between the work library's read and `IFSelect_WorkSession::SetModel`. Both are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest: no OCCT code runs on the bytes before they pass. Under (ii) OCCT loads the model first, and finding 3, finding 7 and the instance numbered 2147483648 fail in that load. (i) must follow OCCT's lexer exactly: a rule read another way either misses a cycle or sees one OCCT does not. Unit tests of `step_check.rs` and kernel.md tests 177 to 186, 189 and 190 pin each rule. The check is read against `step.lex` again whenever the OCCT pin moves.
4. *Rust or C++.* (i) A Rust module called by `read_step` before the shim; (ii) C++ in `cpp/exchange.cpp`, after the first poll under the STEP mutex. Both are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest: it is memory safe on untrusted bytes, a panic in it is a fuzz finding, and libFuzzer instruments it, which gives `step_read` coverage guidance over the bytes (delivery.md open issue 22 (g)). It runs outside the mutex and needs no new `.cpp` file. Its costs: kernel.md 4.1 gains a row for the Rust check, and 3.13 item 2 says that it runs before the first poll. A requested interrupt does not stop a call whose bytes the check refuses. Test 136's `b"hello"` and every valid file still reach OCCT under the mutex (tests 136, 160 and 165).
5. *Finding 3, 5e3efd71.* (i) A check of the syntax of every parameter list in every section, with a patch that makes `LoadedEntity` skip a record without an entity; (ii) a check of the header section alone; (iii) the `LoadedEntity` patch alone; (iv) refusing empty parameters alone; (v) the check of (i) without the patch. All five are open source. The rule takes (v). The probes above show the same SEGV from a data entity, which (ii) misses, and from `PT(O(1),(FOO,(1)))`, which (iv) misses. Under (iii) the file reads with `EmptyResult` (ADR 0067's note), test 174 cannot pass, and the reads of uninitialized header records that valgrind reported for this input stay. (i) and (v) are as safe on every input known: every input shown to reach the null of `LoadedEntity`, finding 3, the probes above, the instance numbered 2147483648 and the second pass of finding 7, is refused by the check before OCCT reads it. No test can then show what the `LoadedEntity` change does, and the patch would ship an untested change to OCCT for review and for upstream. (v) is the industry standard of a patch to a dependency: each change comes with an input that shows it, which the project's rule that untested code does not merge asks too. A first form of the patch held the change. It left the patch in the track's review. The check of (v) follows OCCT's grammar for a parameter list: `(`, then nothing or parameters separated by commas, then `)`. A parameter is a reference, a value of one token, a list, or a word followed by a list. A list starts at a `(` right after a word. Parentheses outside a list are left to OCCT, whose recovery skips a broken instance and records a fail (finding 1, test 169). Every file it refuses breaks ISO 10303-21, or is one that OCCT's lexer misreads: a string with a doubled apostrophe right before `,` or `)` ends at that apostrophe for OCCT. OCCT's parser records a syntax fail for each such list (kernel.md read item 7, open issue 19).
6. *Finding 5, 51283b77.* The options of delivery.md 3.8 (ADR 0067 choice 9) and one more: (a) a patch to OCCT 7.9.3, applied by `scripts/build-occt.sh` and offered upstream; (b) `OSD::SetSignal`; (c) the reader in a process of its own on the native hosts; (d) a newer OCCT release with the fix; (e) a check of the bytes by entity type, which refuses an `EDGE_CURVE` whose end is not a `VERTEX_POINT`. The rule takes (a), the most open-source: the fix goes into OCCT's own code, for everyone who uses it, and technical design section 5 asks to "track upstream patches in our fork". It is also the safest that works in the web build. (b) turns a signal into an exception, which leaves OCCT's state undefined after a null read, kernel.md 3.9 item 7 rules it out, and WebAssembly has no signals. (c) has no second process in the web build. (d) finds no release with a fix: OCCT's `master` of 2026-11-15 has the same `AreEndsMatch`, and a move to OCCT 8.0 is a separate decision under ADR 0003. (e) mirrors one site of OCCT's type rules in the check and needs type-aware reading of complex instances. Needs human review at the M2 gate.
   - *What the patch changes.* (a1) A fail in `RWStepShape_RWEdgeCurve::Check` and a null check in `AreEndsMatch`; (a2) the load type of both ends in `ReadStep`, `StepShape_VertexPoint` in place of `StepShape_Vertex`; (a3) null checks in `AreEndsMatch` and in `StepToTopoDS_TranslateVertex::Init`; (a4) (a1) with `VerifyCheckList` mended to add a check of its own for each entity, as `CompleteCheckList` does. All four are open source. The rule takes (a2), the safest that works. (a1) was built and measured: `VerifyCheckList` loses the fail (Context), and test 176 still crashed in the transfer. (a3) stops both crashes, but the file then transfers with a broken edge, and test 176 asks for `ReadFailed`. (a4) makes every semantic fail count, and read item 4 would then refuse `corpus/imported/assets/b6c7d671...`, which Partsmith's own export wrote, for its two `ERROR: non 2-manifold topology`. Under (a2) a plain vertex fails to load, the way any parameter of the wrong type does, and read item 4 refuses it with the load fails it already reads (tests 169 and 170). `AreEndsMatch` and `TranslateVertex` then only ever see vertex points and need no change. The plain `VERTEX` is valid in ISO 10303-42's topology schema, but OCCT's transfer cannot read an edge curve without points, and AP214's rules for a B-rep ask for vertex points. The probe of the Note found no file but `51283b77` that gains a fail.
7. *The form of the patch.* (i) One patch file, `scripts/occt-7.9.3.patch`, applied with `git apply` to `build-occt.sh`'s fresh clone; (ii) a fork of OCCT with a tag of its own. Both are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest and the industry standard of projects that patch a pinned dependency: the diff stays small and readable, the pin stays the upstream tag, and a pin move must redo the patch, since its name holds the version. (ii) moves the pin to a repository Partsmith keeps. Needs human review at the M2 gate.
   - *Line endings.* The repository checks the patch out with LF (`* text=auto eol=lf`), and OCCT's own `.gitattributes` checks its `.cxx` files out with LF. `git apply` then finds the same bytes on every runner, Git Bash on Windows included.
   - *An existing src.* (a) No patch for any existing `src`; (b) the patch for an unchanged clone of the pinned commit, a Git clone whose checked-out commit is `OCCT_COMMIT` and whose tracked files are that commit's, and none for any other `src`; (c) the patch for every existing `src`. All three are open source. The rule takes (b), the safest. A first form took (a), and the track's review showed what it missed: `scripts/occt-source-archive.sh` and every earlier run of `build-occt.sh` leave such a clone in the default work folder, and building again built it without the patch, an install that crashes tests 176 and 188. (c) breaks the rule of delivery.md 2.13 that a modified `src` or an extracted archive is built as it is, which is how a user builds a modified OCCT. Under (b) a clone the patch was applied to is a modified `src`, and a second run builds it as it is (test 156).
   - *The archive.* (i) The tag's tree as before, and the patch file added after it with `git archive --add-file`; (ii) the patched tree. The rule takes (i), the safest: the archive still names the pinned commit, which `scripts/check-source-archive.sh` and `deploy` read, and the same pin and patch give the same bytes. (ii) has no commit to name. The relink commands of the notices apply the patch with `patch -d <work>/src -p1 -i occt-7.9.3.patch` after the extraction.
   - *The license of the patch file* (needs human review at the M2 gate). The file changes OCCT's code and is under OCCT's license, the GNU Lesser General Public License version 2.1 with the Open CASCADE exception, as its header says. The rest of the repository stays Apache-2.0 (ADR 0028).
   - *The offer upstream.* The patch is written in OCCT's style, formatted with OCCT's `.clang-format`, and offered to OCCT upstream. The offer goes to the owner's report. OCCT's process for contributions may ask for a contributor agreement. Nobody is contacted.
8. *The raw messages.* (i) One message per rule, in technical words, naming the instance or the line; (ii) one message for every rule. Both are open source. The rule takes (i), the recommendation: each rule's message says what to look for in the file, as the check list's messages of read item 4 do. The messages are `the STEP file breaks STEP's syntax inside a parameter list on line <n>`, `the STEP file's instance number #<digits> is larger than 2147483647`, `the STEP file defines the instance number #<n> more than once, again on line <l>`, `the STEP file opens or closes a scope on line <l>`, `the STEP file's instance references form a cycle: #<h> reaches itself through <k> references` (`1 reference` when k is 1) and `the STEP file's instance references form a chain of more than 500 references from #<h>`. Only `KernelError::detail()` shows them. The user message and the hint stay those of kernel.md 4.3.
9. *A number defined more than once.* (i) A reference to it leads to each of its instances, and the walk goes on; (ii) a reference leads to the one OCCT picks, the nearest before the reference, else the nearest after it; (iii) the check refuses the file at the second definition. All three are open source. The rule takes (iii), the safest: OCCT's second pass, which a number defined twice starts, reads memory it never set and hung the fuzzer (finding 7). (i) and (ii) let that pass run. A first form took (i). OCCT already gave a number defined twice outside a scope the fail `Ident defined SEVERAL TIMES`, which read item 4 refuses. (iii) therefore refuses no file that read before. The walk needs no group nodes for repeated numbers. Kernel.md test 186 now states the refusal at the second definition.
10. *OCCT's lost semantic checks.* (i) Record them (kernel.md open issue 20) and leave `VerifyCheckList` as it is; (ii) mend it in the patch. Both are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest: mended, read item 4 would refuse a tracked corpus file that imports today (choice 6, (a4)). ADR 0067 choice 10 assumed that the semantic fails counted. Its option (i) stands, since the load fails, which hold every crash measured so far, still count. The report of the fault to OCCT goes to the owner's report with the patch.
11. *Finding 6, the recovered cycle of oriented edges (1274c3a5).* (a) A change to the patch: `RWStepShape_RWOrientedEdge::ReadStep` fails an oriented edge whose edge element is an oriented edge (ISO 10303-42 oriented_edge WR1); (b) a stronger check of the bytes that also checks each data entity's form outside its parameter lists (`#n = TYPE(...)` or a complex instance) and refuses the malformed file; (c) a depth bound patched into `StepShape_OrientedEdge::EdgeStart` and `EdgeEnd`. All three are open source. The rule takes (a), the safest. The cycle is not in the bytes: OCCT's error recovery builds it, and the check of choice 1 cannot see it. (a) enforces the schema's WR1, by which an oriented edge's edge element is never an oriented edge. `EdgeStart` and `EdgeEnd` then recurse at most one step, whatever the recovery builds. It is also a second line behind the check for finding 2's oriented edge of itself. (b) would mirror OCCT's grammar outside the parameter lists, which kernel.md read item 7 leaves to OCCT ("Parentheses outside a parameter list ... are not checked") for finding 1's broken line. It would also refuse files that OCCT's recovery reads today. (c) changes the shared `StepShape_OrientedEdge` and bounds one recursion of several. With (a), `ReadStream` reads the file to its end, and read item 4 refuses it for the parse fails of the recovery (test 188). Needs human review at the M2 gate.
12. *Scopes.* (i) The check refuses `&SCOPE` and `ENDSCOPE`; (ii) it refuses only an export list after `ENDSCOPE`, and leaves scopes to OCCT; (iii) it leaves both to OCCT, as a first form did. All three are open source. The rule takes (i), the safest. A scope is the one place where ISO 10303-21 lets a file define a number again, and choice 9 refuses that. OCCT does not process an export list, and its parser prints a warning for one to standard output, past the messenger (kernel.md section 3.9 item 7). The track's review found that print in a probe: the line came after the probe's own output on standard output, the stream where `partsmith rebuild --json` writes its JSON. (ii) stops the print but keeps scopes, whose numbers choice 9 already refuses to repeat, and OCCT's code for them. (iii) keeps the print. No file Git tracks, no export and neither of OCCT's two STEP files holds a scope (the Note), and Partsmith's writer makes none. Kernel.md test 190 states the refusal, and `standard_streams` of `exchange.rs` reads the export list in its child process.

## Consequences

- kernel.md: section 1's bullet, section 3.8.1 read item 4's note and read items 6 to 8, 3.13 item 2, a row of 4.1, tests 173 to 190 and open issues 19 and 20. delivery.md: 2.1's files, 2.9's two statements and the wrapping rule, 2.13's archive and clone, 2.15's corpus and measurements, 3.2's keys, 3.8's findings 2 to 7, tests 55, 56, 156 to 158, a row of section 4, open issue 22 and section 8. ADR 0003 and ADR 0034 gain an amendment row.
- The first `ci.yml` run on `main` misses every OCCT cache: `occt-native`, `occt-wasm` and `occt-platforms` rebuild, about 56, 88, 48 and 20 minutes (ADR 0039's figures), within their 150 minutes. The nightly `fuzz` job restores the new native key. A night before that run fails the row on a cache miss. The archive's SHA-256 changes, and `OCCT_SOURCE_SHA256` holds the new one.
- A STEP file from another program that holds a cycle, a chain longer than 500 references, a number defined more than once, a scope, a broken parameter list or an edge curve without vertex points no longer imports. Each of them crashed, hung, printed or misread before. Files from other programs are barely sampled (kernel.md open issue 19).

## Note (2026-11-15): the measurements

- **The rules of choices 9 and 12.** A scan of the 8 STEP files Git tracks and of OCCT's two files found no number defined twice and no `&SCOPE` or `ENDSCOPE`: 18,623 definitions in `linkrods.step`, 1,239 in `screw.step` and 350 to 1,482 in each tracked file.
