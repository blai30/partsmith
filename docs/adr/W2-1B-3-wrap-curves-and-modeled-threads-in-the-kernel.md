# W2-1B-3: Wrap curves and modeled threads in the kernel

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05); needs human review at the M2 gate for its change to the kernel facade's public API, the rule of [ADR 0026](0026-kernel-facade-additions-for-errors.md): the calls `wrap_curves` and `modeled_thread`, the types `WrapSurface`, `Helix`, `WrapCurve`, `WrapPiece`, `WrapResult`, `ThreadTooth`, `ThreadResult` and `ToothVertex`, the enums `HelixHand`, `ThreadKind`, `ToothSide`, `ToothFlank`, `ToothFace` and `ToothEdge`, and `Operation::WrapCurves` and `Operation::ModeledThread`. partsmith-features and naming will match on the enums. It specifies the kernel calls of the M2 plan's items 19 and 20 in kernel.md sections 2.15 and 3.4.14, from the thread spike's construction that [ADR 0074](0074-modeled-threads-lofted-between-helical-wires-and-divided-per-turn.md) takes. It changes no file format, protocol schema or naming algorithm and adds no dependency: `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` is in `TKOffset`, `ShapeUpgrade_ShapeDivideAngle` in `TKShHealing` and `BRepLib` in `TKTopAlgo`, all linked (kernel.md section 3.12 item 3).

## Context

- **ADR 0074** takes section 8.2's `lofted-divided`: each helix a `Geom2d_Line` on the parameter plane of a cylinder or a cone, one edge per turn, a 3D curve from `BRepLib::BuildCurve3d` within 1e-7 mm, a ruled solid `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` between the inner and outer wires, `ShapeUpgrade_ShapeDivideAngle(2 pi)` with the frame turned to the start, then the fuse or cut of kernel.md section 3.4.5. Its decision 4 asks that a modeled thread's failure message offer the cosmetic thread.
- **One code path** (kernel.md section 8.3's Feeds). The thread's map and the emboss's map are the same: a cylinder or a cone, its developed plane and its parameter plane. The coil of item 19 sweeps along the same exact helix (spike 8.8's coil (i)). Item 31's emboss adds arcs and B-splines of the developed plane in wave 4.
- **The spike's API** (`docs/design/spikes/helix-thread.patch`) took a signed pitch for the hand, gave the helix of `wrap_curves` one edge, built every construction it tried behind one call, and reported its roles as a list of (element, part, piece, history source).
- **Two defects** (kernel.md open issue 22). `ThruSections::Generated` names the lines of a flank one piece late when the section wire walks an edge reversed: section 8.2's wire did so on its upper helix, and the start's upper line had no generator and the end's had two. `Shape::is_valid()` accepts a boolean that lost an argument: plain `swept` kept 0.05531 mm³ of a 728.91 mm³ rod and stayed valid.
- **What was measured.** Right-hand teeth on bases already at the tooth's inner radius, ending P/4 inside each end. Base preparation, run-out and left-hand threads were not (spike 8.9, kernel.md section 8.9).
- **The plan's choices** for naming threads (track 3A) and for detecting a boolean that dropped an argument recommend flank lines named by their faces and a build-time check of solids and volume. Track 3A decides the names. The check needs the volumes and history the kernel holds.

## Decision

1. **One parameter-plane map.** `WrapSurface` (a cylinder, or a cone with its half-angle) and `wrap_curves(surface, curves)` with `WrapCurve::Segment`, exact on a cylinder only, and `WrapCurve::Helix`. `WrapCurve` derives `Clone` and not `Copy`: the emboss's arcs and B-splines can join it in wave 4 without taking `Copy` away from its callers. `wrap_curves` returns one wire per curve with each edge's and vertex's curve and piece (`WrapResult`). The coil's taper is its cone (the decisions of 2026-11-19, item 6).
2. **The hand is a field.** `Helix { phase, height, pitch, turns, hand }`, with a positive pitch and `HelixHand::Right` or `Left`. A helix always rises from `height`, of either hand.
3. **One edge per turn on a turned frame.** Every helix, of `wrap_curves` as of the thread, starts at angle 0 of its surface turned by its phase, and its edges end on the surface's seam at every whole turn, as ADR 0074 decision 1 does for the thread.
4. **One call for the thread.** `modeled_thread(base, tooth, kind, interrupt)` builds ADR 0074's tooth from a `ThreadTooth` (a surface, depth, two widths and the helix of its inner lower corner) and joins it by the fuse (`External`) or the cut (`Internal`) of section 3.4.5. Section 8.2 measured the construction with its boolean as one step.
5. **Section wires with every edge forward.** Each side's wire builds every edge in the direction the wire walks it. `ThruSections` then binds and resolves each vertex the same way. This settles kernel.md open issue 21 (a) for the kernel. Test 231 checks every flank line.
6. **Roles and generators on the tooth.** `ThreadResult` reports a role for every face and a generator for every edge and vertex of the tooth, in `Crest` and `Root` terms that the kind decides, and the join's history (inputs [base, tooth]) carries them to the result. The lines the division adds are found by adjacency, never by position.
7. **The kernel checks that the join kept both.** After the join, a face of the result must come from each argument and the volumes must bound the result. A failure is `StdFail_NotDone` (kind `Failed`), whose hint offers the cosmetic thread.
8. **Stage 1 takes section 8.2's construction.** Base preparation and run-out come from spike 8.9 through track 2B. Left-hand teeth are specified now and measured by spikes 8.8 and 8.9 before the code is written (spike 8.9 gains a left-hand input).
9. **No lock.** `ThruSections` and `ShapeDivideAngle` run without a mutex: OCCT 7.9.3's sources hold no static that keeps state between calls. Test 234 runs the thread on 8 threads.

## Alternatives considered

Every option is open source, built on OCCT. The first criterion of the rule picks none of them. The rule then takes the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *The map's types.* Options: (i) one `WrapSurface` and one `WrapCurve` for the helix, the thread and the emboss; (ii) separate helix and thread types, with the emboss's own later. The rule takes (i), the safest: section 8.3's Feeds ask for one code path, and two would drift. `Clone` without `Copy` follows the lesson of `PlanarCurve`, which lost `Copy` when it gained B-splines.
2. *The hand.* Options: (i) a `HelixHand` field and a positive pitch; (ii) the spike's signed pitch. The rule takes (i), the safest: with (ii) a left-hand helix that rises must start at its top, and a sign is easy to lose in a feature's arithmetic. Fusion's Coil and Thread and FreeCAD's Helix offer the hand as a choice of its own, the industry standard.
3. *The helix's edges.* Options: (i) one edge per turn, the turns ending on the seam; (ii) one edge per helix, as the spike's `wrap_curves` built it. The rule takes (i), the safest: section 8.2 found faces over many periods misread by later booleans and by `Shape::volume`, and spike 8.8's coil (i) sweeps along edges per turn.
4. *The thread's calls.* Options: (i) one call that builds and joins; (ii) a tooth call, with features calling `fuse` or `cut`; (iii) one call per kind. The rule takes (i), the safest: the check of decision 7 needs the tooth, the base and the join together, section 8.2 measured them as one step, and its poll gap is the cancel budget's. (iii) would repeat the tooth.
5. *The flank lines* (kernel.md open issue 21 (a)). Options: (i) section wires with every edge forward; (ii) the shim corrects `ThruSections`' list by one piece; (iii) no generator for the lines, and naming names them by their two faces. The rule takes (i), the safest: it removes the cause in the shim's own input, where (ii) depends on OCCT's defect staying as it is. Naming may still pick (iii) for its names (track 3A).
6. *Where the generators sit.* Options: (i) on the tooth, with the join's history; (ii) on the result's elements; (iii) in inner and outer terms. The rule takes (i), the safest: every tooth element has exactly one generator, a test can check that whole, and the join's history follows kernel.md section 3.3 item 7 as every boolean does. Crest and root are the words of naming.md section 3.12.1 and of every thread standard.
7. *Detecting a lost argument.* Options: (i) the kernel's check after the join, failing with `Failed`; (ii) tests only; (iii) a check after every boolean of the facade; (iv) a new error kind. The rule takes (i), the safest: a vanished thread never reaches a body, and the hint gives the way forward. (ii) lets it ship. (iii) costs every boolean two volumes, where no other loss has been seen. (iv) adds a public kind for a failure whose fix is the same as any other failed thread's. This is the plan's choice "Detecting a boolean that dropped an argument", listed for stage 3 (track 3A's ADR), taken here as the decisions of 2026-11-19 take it: the plan's recommendation (ii), a build-time check of solids and volume, is (i) above, placed in the kernel because the kernel holds the volumes and the history. Track 3A's ADR words the feature's message and does not decide the check again.
