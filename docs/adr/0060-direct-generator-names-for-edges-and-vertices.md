# 0060: Direct-generator names for edges and vertices, as additions and aliases

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the M2 plan's choice 3, option (b), and the choices below. It changes the naming algorithm (naming.md sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.5, 3.6, 3.8 to 3.12). It changes what a file format version 3 document may hold: a reference's `q` may be a direct-generator name, with no new field and no schema change. Both go to human review at the M2 gate (AGENTS.md Process, m2-plan.md gate 19). No protocol schema, dependency or license changes. It extends [ADR 0021](0021-resolve-references-by-meaning.md)'s decision "Meaning, never guesses" by one more source of results, the direct-generator names that kernel history carries (naming.md section 3.12.4), and does not reopen it. Fingerprints still choose an element only as a reported guess. [ADR 0022](0022-split-regions-keep-their-area.md) and [ADR 0023](0023-frames-live-on-the-model.md) stand: direct-generator names follow no succession and carry no motion.

## Context

- **M1 names every edge and vertex by its faces.** naming.md section 3.3 steps 4 and 5 name an edge `edge(<face a>|<face b>)` and a vertex by the faces that contain it. An edge or vertex without an adjacent face stays unnamed, and so does a degenerated edge. Where the same faces meet more than once, the elements are numbered in geometric order (`edge(f-a/x|f-b/y)#2`, section 3.4). naming.md open issue 4 records that no edge has a direct generator in M1, although architecture.md's "Topological naming" speaks of "an edge without a direct generator".
- **M1's order-dependent edges and vertices.** In M1 every body is a solid. Only degenerated edges stay unnamed there. Many M1 names are order dependent through their faces' `#n`, such as the edges of a split face. Of the elements that a generator of their own feature could name, the face rule numbers the edges and vertices where two lateral faces of one extrude or revolve meet twice (a line and an arc that close a D shape), the two poles of a revolve whose profile curve meets the axis at both its ends, the two poles of a sphere and the two seams of a torus. No example under `examples/` and no scenario of [the reference stability corpus](../design/reference-stability-corpus.md) stores a reference to one of them at a3f4573. Only tests read the sphere's and the torus's numbered names: `crates/partsmith-recompute/tests/naming_conformance.rs`, `crates/partsmith-features/tests/descriptions.rs` and `crates/partsmith-naming/tests/naming_maps.rs`.
- **M2 brings elements that the face rule names weakly or not at all.** A helix (item 19) can be a curve without faces. A thread's crest and root (item 20) run between flank faces that can split per turn. A sweep along several path segments (item 18) has rails between lateral faces split per segment. Projected sketch geometry (item 26) stores body edges and vertices as references, and their names decide how well it follows upstream edits.
- **The kernel already reports edge and vertex history.** kernel.md section 3.3 lists, for every face, edge and vertex of every input of an operation that returns `OpResult`, its `modified` images and the elements it `generated` (`fate_of` in `crates/partsmith-occt-sys/cpp/history.cpp`). M1 naming reads the faces' `modified` lists (`modified_sources` in `crates/partsmith-features/src/naming.rs`) and, for fillet, chamfer and shell, the `generated` lists of faces, edges and vertices (`add_origins` in `crates/partsmith-features/src/builtin/dressup.rs`). It never reads the `modified` lists of edges and vertices. It derives edge and vertex names again from the face names in every map (`NamingMap::build` in `crates/partsmith-naming/src/map/build.rs`). `SweepResult`, which extrude and revolve return, carries face roles and no history.
- **The plan settled the rule.** The M2 plan's choice 3 picked option (b): direct-generator names only for edges and vertices that M1 leaves unnamed or names in a weaker form, aliases where both forms exist, no renaming of an element M1 names, and no migration. Its reason: every M1 reference resolves unchanged, no migration rewrites content, and the alias idea that merged faces use carries over. This ADR records that choice and settles the choices it leaves open.
- **The character set.** The JSON Schema of `ElementName` has the pattern `^[A-Za-z0-9_./()|#@:-]+$` (naming.md section 2.2, `docs/format/ptsm.schema.json`, `docs/format/protocol.schema.json`). A feature id is `[A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64}`. It holds neither `(` nor `:`.

## Decision

1. **The form.** A direct-generator name is `edge:` or `vertex:` followed by a body of the form of a face name whose generator is a sketch reference or absent: `<feature>/<role>[/<sketch-ref>]`, then `#n` and `@<feature>:<instance>` groups (naming.md section 3.1 rule 7). Its prefix gives its kind. It never stands as the generator of another name and never in a face list. `ElementName::direct`, `is_direct` and `direct_parts` build and read it (section 2.2). Examples: `edge:f-hel1/helix`, `vertex:f-hel1/start`, `edge:f-swp1/rail/e-1.end#2`, `edge:f-hel1/helix@f-lp1:1`.
2. **Which elements get one.** A feature passes a `DirectSource` for each edge and vertex: `New` with the direct-generator name it made the element from, `From` the input elements it descends from, or `None` (section 2.5). A `New` is given only to an element that the face rule leaves unnamed (and that is not a degenerated edge) or names with a `#` (section 3.12.3). A direct-generator name, once given, is carried by `From` in every later map, whatever the face-rule name becomes. Duplicates are numbered in the order of section 3.4. An element without a face-rule name is named by its first direct-generator name. Every other element keeps its face-rule name, and its direct-generator names are its aliases. They live on the element, apart from the merge aliases of section 2.6 (section 3.12.2, choice 9).
3. **No M1 feature makes one in M2.** Their rules in section 3.6 pass `From` and `None` only (section 3.12.3, choice 6 below).
4. **Carried by kernel history.** Every feature rule passes, for each edge and vertex of a kernel result with history, `From` the input elements of its kind whose `modified` list contains it, else `None` (section 3.6). Copies, moves and splits carry them through `with_instance`, `moved` and `project` (section 3.5). `NamingMap::build_with_direct` takes the sources. `NamingMap::build` passes none and builds the map M1 builds (step 9 of section 3.3).
5. **Resolution order.** A query resolves by its form. A direct-generator query resolves by section 3.12.4 against the direct-generator names the elements hold: the element that holds it (`Exact`, or `History` with `Merged` for a name that merged into an element holding an earlier one), its pieces (`History`), the guess rule for an order-dependent query, a lost piece, or a loss. Any other query resolves by section 3.9 steps 1 to 5 against element names, unchanged. The two never meet. A direct-generator query reads no aliases, successions, anchors or merged names, and has no continuation or relatives.
6. **Picks.** `Reference::from_element` writes the element's direct-generator name as `q` when the element has no face-rule name, or when its face-rule name is order dependent and its first direct-generator name is not. Otherwise it writes the reference of section 3.11, unchanged (section 3.12.5). `ElementInfo::reference_name` gives that `q`. A reference with a direct-generator `q` takes no `nb` and no `merged`. Engine listings send the same `q` (engine.md section 3.9, by handoff).
7. **Fingerprints** are those of section 3.7. An edge or vertex without adjacent faces has `adj=0,X` (section 3.12.6).
8. **Format.** No new field. A version 3 document may hold direct-generator names in a reference's `q`. The schemas of `ElementName`, `Reference`, `Anchors` and `Merged` do not change. A version 2 document never holds one. M1 builds write none.
9. **The contract for later waves.** Helix and coil (item 19), thread (item 20) and sweep (item 18) use the role words of naming.md section 3.12.1 (`helix`, `start`, `end`, `rail`, `crest`, `root`). Each of their specs confirms them, adds its face rule to section 3.6 and names the kernel result that reports its generators from OCCT's own history (`Generated`, `FirstShape`, `LastShape`), never from positions. Projection (item 26) stores its source edges and vertices as picks store them (choice 10). Loft, rib, web and emboss may give direct-generator names under the same section.

## Options considered

The plan's choice 3 is settled and recorded here: (a) rename every edge after its direct generator, with a migration that rewrites stored references; (b) direct-generator names only where M1 leaves an element unnamed or names it in a weaker form, with aliases and no renaming; (c) per-feature edge names with no general rule. The plan picked (b): every M1 reference resolves unchanged, and no migration rewrites content.

**The choices the owner's rule settles.** Each is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05). Every option is code in this repository with no dependency. None is more open than another. The rule then takes the safest.

1. *The written form.* The options: (i) `edge:` or `vertex:` and a body of the form of a face name; (ii) a generator inside the M1 form, such as `edge(f-hel1/helix)`; (iii) a field of its own on references (`"g"`) beside `q`; (iv) a face-shaped name with an edge role, such as `f-hel1/helix-edge`. The rule takes (i), the safest. It needs no schema change: the pattern already allows `:`. It cannot collide with an M1 name: a feature id holds neither `(` nor `:`. It reuses the face name's parser, its pieces and its copies. Under (ii) `edge(f-hel1/helix)` already means the seam of a face named `f-hel1/helix`. Under (iii) both schemas change, and two names that disagree need a rule. Under (iv) the kind would come from a role word, against naming.md section 3.1 rule 4, and a role could clash with a face's. It is also the industry standard: FreeCAD 1.0's element maps name a generated element after what generated it, as Partsmith's face names already do.
2. *What a weaker form is.* The options: (i) no face-rule name, or one that holds `#` anywhere; (ii) only a face-rule name with its own trailing `#n`; (iii) every element with a generator gets a direct-generator name, and picks use it only where it is stronger. The rule takes (i), the safest. Any `#` makes a name's meaning depend on geometric order (section 3.1). Under (ii) the rail of a multi-segment sweep, `edge(f-swp1/lateral/e-1#1|f-swp1/lateral/e-2#1)`, would keep its order-dependent name although the sweep knows its profile point. Under (iii) maps would carry names that no pick writes.
3. *How direct-generator names reach later maps.* The options: (i) by the kernel's edge and vertex history, in every feature; (ii) keyed by the face-rule name, looked up again in each later map; (iii) made again by each feature. The rule takes (i), the safest. History follows the element itself. Under (ii) a name would be lost whenever a face splits, merges or follows a succession, the very cases the names are for. Under (iii) a fillet cannot know what a helix made. It is also the industry standard: OCCT's `Modified` history, which naming already reads for faces.
4. *Which name a pick writes.* The options: (i) the direct-generator name where it is the element's only name, or where the face-rule name is order dependent and the first direct-generator name is not; (ii) always the face-rule name; (iii) the direct-generator name whenever the element holds one. The rule takes (i), the safest. Under (ii) a pick of a thread's crest would be as fragile as M1's numbered names. Under (iii) a pick would lose the face history that a face-rule name without `#` follows: merges, successions, the rims of shells and relatives. A fresh pick under (i) resolves `Exact`.
5. *What a generator can be.* The options: (i) a sketch reference, or none for the feature's own construction; (ii) also an element name, as face names allow. The rule takes (i), the safest and the least code. Under (ii) a generator renamed by an upstream change would need the canonical matching of section 3.9 for direct-generator names too. No M2 feature needs it. A later ADR can add it.
6. *M1's order-dependent elements.* The options: (i) none of them gets a direct-generator name in M2; (ii) the junction points of extrude and revolve profiles, which needs vertex tags in profiles and the edges `BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism` and `BRepPrimAPI_MakeRevol` generate from them; (iii) the poles of a sphere and the seams of a torus, which needs new primitive roles from the kernel. The rule takes (i), the safest. A junction's sketch point changes its id when the user replaces either entity that meets there. A sketch succession follows the lateral face of a replaced entity, not its points (naming.md section 3.6.2). Under (ii) a pick would then be lost where the face-rule name follows the succession. Under (iii) the kernel grows for elements that no example and no corpus scenario references. The anchors and the guess rule of section 3.9 keep covering them. A later ADR may add (ii) with successions for sketch points.
7. *Anchors and merged names on direct-generator references.* The options: (i) none, with pieces under the guess rule and every source's name kept on a merged element; (ii) anchors from the faces an element touches, as for face-rule pieces. The rule takes (i), the safest and the least code. A wire's edges touch no face. Under (ii) they would have no anchors where direct-generator names matter most.
8. *How native and WebAssembly results are compared.* The options: (i) run the naming crate's tests on `wasm32-unknown-emscripten` in CI's `kernel-wasm` job, and send each element's reference name in `list_elements`, which engine test 154 compares; (ii) rely on engine test 154 as it is. The rule takes (i), the safest. Engine test 154 compares element names, and an alias is no element's name. A host that keyed a listed element and its pick by `q` would also see two values for one element under (ii).
9. *Where an element's aliases live.* The options: (i) a sorted list on the element (`NamingMap::direct`, `ElementInfo::direct`); (ii) entries in the map's merge `Aliases`, keyed by the direct-generator name and aimed at the face-rule name. The rule takes (i), the safest. `Aliases` holds face names only. Its lists are chased through face families, and its JSON checks require face names (naming.md section 2.6). Under (ii) an edge name would enter face families. A merge's pruning would also drop an alias whose element still exists: step 6 of naming.md section 3.3 keeps only targets that an output face extends. The plan's word "alias" holds: one element answers to several names. The type that holds them is the element's own list.
10. *Projected sketch geometry (item 26).* The options: (i) no direct-generator names: a projected entity lives in a sketch, which has no naming map, and the projection stores each source edge or vertex as a pick stores it (naming.md section 3.12.5); (ii) names for projected entities derived from their source elements. The rule takes (i), the safest and the least code. A projected entity is named from what made it through the reference it stores, and faces built from it are named after its entity id (naming.md section 3.6.2), as the M2 plan's item 2 asks. Under (ii) a sketch would need a naming map of its own, and a projected entity would answer to two identities, its entity id and its derived name.
11. *Examples that an M2 ADR re-blesses, in the baseline of naming.md test 196.* The options: (i) their files leave the baseline, and the re-blessing ADR's dated entry names them; (ii) their files are recorded again once, by the change that re-blesses them. The rule takes (i), the safest. The baseline then only ever holds what the code of a3f4573 gave, and no M2 code writes it. Under (ii) the code of naming.md section 3.12 could write the very entries meant to check it, when it lands before the re-blessing change. m2-plan.md gate 5 already leaves those examples out of "the same results as at 633eeb9".

## Consequences

- Every M1 document, example and corpus scenario resolves as at a3f4573. No M1 feature makes a direct-generator name, every face-rule name stays, and a map without direct-generator names serializes as before. naming.md test 196 pins it against a baseline recorded with the M1 code. The examples that an M2 ADR re-blesses leave that baseline (m2-plan.md gate 5, choice 11).
- partsmith-naming gains `ElementName::direct`, `is_direct` and `direct_parts`, `DirectSource` and `DirectSources`, `NamingMap::build_with_direct` and `direct`, `ElementInfo::direct` and `reference_name`, `NameSet::carries`, three `NamingError` variants and four reason texts (naming.md sections 2 and 4). `ElementInfo` gains a field. The struct literals of the crate's tests gain it too. `NamingMap`'s cache JSON gains an optional `direct` key.
- partsmith-features passes direct sources with every kernel result it names: `name_shape` takes them and `direct_sources` computes them from history (features.md sections 2.11, 3.9 and 3.12, by handoff). Recompute's loss diagnosis reads a lost direct-generator query through `NameSet::carries` (recompute.md section 3.10, by handoff). Engine listings send each element's reference name (engine.md section 3.9, by handoff). The kernel's history for edges and vertices gets acceptance tests of its own (kernel.md section 3.3, by handoff). CI runs the naming tests under WebAssembly (delivery.md section 2.4, by handoff). file-format.md's version 3 and architecture.md's naming text name the new form (by handoff).
- Picks of M2 elements can store `edge:` and `vertex:` names. Documents that hold them are version 3, which M1 builds refuse with `NewerFormatVersion` before they could misread one.
- A fillet or chamfer of an edge that holds an alias still names its face after the face-rule name (section 3.6.5). A rounded face of an order-dependent edge keeps an order-dependent name.
- naming.md section 5 gains three budget rows: `build_with_direct` 7 ms, `ElementSet::new` with direct-generator names 4 ms and a direct-generator `resolve` 0.6 ms. These are placeholders, measured by nothing yet. The change that lands the code sets each from its measured median plus 20 percent ([ADR 0057](0057-budgets-set-from-the-measured-median-plus-20-percent.md) decision 1). The web budgets are three times those. The other rows keep their workloads and budgets.
- Gate 6's scenarios for direct-generator names need a feature that makes them. They come with items 18 to 20 in wave 2, with the naming metric of item 9.
- This changes the naming algorithm and what version 3 documents may hold. Both go to human review at the M2 gate.
