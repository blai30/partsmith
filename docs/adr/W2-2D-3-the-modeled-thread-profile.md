# W2-2D-3: The modeled thread profile

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the M2 plan's open choice "Which profile is modeled, and the FDM truncation", decided by the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 ([m2-wave2-decisions.md](../design/m2-wave2-decisions.md) section 2 item 5), and the print.md rule on thread flanks that section 2 item 16 asks for. It changes the public API of partsmith-print: `AnalysisOptions` gains the member `thread_flanks`, and the constant `THREAD_FLANK_ANGLE` is new. `AnalysisOptions` is not in the protocol schema or the file format. Neither changes. Needs human review at the M2 gate for the truncation's fraction and its reading of "crest and root truncation" (decision 4), and for `THREAD_FLANK_ANGLE`, a print value the technical design does not give (print.md section 8). No naming algorithm, license or dependency change.

## Context

- **Technical design section 11** asks for "FDM-tuned thread profiles (enlarged clearance, truncated crests), modeled threads by default". m2-plan.md item 20 repeats it. kernel.md section 8.2 built its threads on ISO 68-1's basic profile with 0.2 mm of radial clearance and left "the print truncation" to features/thread.md.
- **The wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19** (section 2 item 5): threads model the maximum-material limit of their class, by ISO 965-1's fundamental deviations and ASME B1.1's allowance computed by formula; tolerance widths are not modeled; the clearance per side and "an explicit crest and root truncation that does not depend on the technology: a stated fraction of P beyond ISO 68-1's flats" come on top, chosen here with its source.
- **Resin.** `printer.nozzle` has no value on a resin printer (print.md section 3.5). Gate 20 prints the threaded cap and a tapped block on FDM and on resin.
- **The kernel's tooth** (kernel.md sections 2.15 and 3.4.14) is a trapezoid in each half-plane through the axis: an inner side on a cylinder, an outer side `depth` farther out, each with a width along the axis. It needs an outer side of at least 1e-6 mm.
- **Overhangs.** A flank of a 60-degree thread makes 30 degrees with a radius. With the axis vertical, the downward flanks overhang at 60 degrees from vertical, past `generic-fdm`'s 45 and `generic-resin`'s 30 (print.md sections 2.2 and 3.9.5). A helix's lead only makes them less steep. Each downward flank spans only the thread's depth `5H / 8` across (0.68 mm for M8x1.25, 3.25 mm for M64x6), and threads print upright without supports on FDM and on resin. On FDM, a 0.2 mm layer steps such a flank out by 0.35 mm, less than a 0.4 mm line. Every modeled thread printed upright would still get overhang findings that advise supports inside the thread.

## Decision

1. **Basic profile.** ISO 68-1's, also ASME B1.1's basic form: flats of P/8 at the major diameter and P/4 at the minor diameter, depth 5H/8, `H = sqrt(3) / 2 P`.
2. **The class's maximum-material limit.** The profile moves inward by half of δ on the radius: δ is ISO 965-1's `-es` of position g, `(15 + 11 P)` µm, for 6g; ASME B1.1's allowance for 2A, 0.300 times the 2A pitch diameter tolerance with the length of engagement equal to the basic major diameter; and 0 for 6H and 2B (`EI = 0`, no allowance).
3. **The clearance.** The profile moves away from its mate by the clearance c on the radius ([ADR W2-2D-2](W2-2D-2-the-printer-clearance-of-threads-and-holes.md)).
4. **The truncation.** Every crest is cut back and every root cut deeper by `t = H / 12 = sqrt(3) / 24 P`, about 0.072 P, on the radius, beyond ISO 68-1's flats. The flanks do not move. The source of the fraction is ISO 965-1's design minor diameter of an external thread, `d3 = d1 - H / 6`: an external root at the maximum-material limit lies `H / 12` below ISO 68-1's flat. The print profile takes that depth for every root, external and internal, and cuts every crest back by the same amount. The external crest's flat widens from P/8 to 5P/24, the internal crest's from P/4 to P/3.

   "Crest and root truncation" is read as material removed at both: a root filled beyond ISO 68-1's flat would cross the maximum-material limit of decision 2 (a 6H root may not lie inside the basic profile) and would meet a standard screw at its maximum material. Cutting the roots deeper keeps every modeled thread inside its class's limit and costs no engagement.
5. **The tooth.** features/thread.md section 5.5 gives the kernel's `ThreadTooth` fields: for an external thread, inner widths `5P/6` and outer `5P/24` at the root radius `D1/2 - δ/2 - c - t`; for an internal one, the groove `2P/3` wide at the bore `D1/2 + c + t` and `P/24` at its bottom; depth 5H/8 for both.
6. **Thread flanks in the print checks.** `AnalysisOptions` gains `thread_flanks: Vec<u32>`, face ids of the analyzed mesh, empty by default and left out of its JSON when empty. The overhang check does not flag a triangle of a listed face whose overhang angle is at most `THREAD_FLANK_ANGLE` (70 degrees, `7 pi / 18`) plus `ANGLE_TOLERANCE`. 70 degrees is a 60-degree thread's flank with its axis vertical plus 10 degrees: the angle between a mesh triangle and its surface reaches the tessellation's angular deviation, 10 degrees at the engine's draft quality (engine.md section 3.7). A steeper triangle of a listed face, from a thread tilted from vertical or a coarse mesh, is flagged as before, with the profile's limit. The overlay keeps every angle. The engine lists the display faces whose element names carry a thread's flank roles (track 3A, in engine.md section 3.12 and naming.md). print.md sections 2.1, 2.5 and 3.9.5 and tests 139 to 141.

## Alternatives considered

Each choice states its options, the pick and the owner's rule's reasons in order: the most open-source approach, the safest, the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Every option is open source. The first criterion picks none of them.

1. *Where in the class the model sits.* (i) The basic profile, with the class only recorded; (ii) the middle of the class's tolerance zone; (iii) the maximum-material limit. The wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 take (iii) (section 2 item 5): it is the limit at which a part still fits any mate of its class, and it needs the fundamental deviation only, no tolerance width.
2. *The truncation's fraction.* (i) `H / 12`, from ISO 965-1's `d3`; (ii) `H / 8`; (iii) a fraction with no standard behind it, such as P/16; (iv) none beyond ISO 68-1, the spike's profile. The wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 rule out (iv). (ii) would make the external crest flat P/4, ISO 68-1's internal crest flat, but leave the internal root no flat at all (`P/8 - 2 t tan 30° = 0`), and the kernel refuses it. Safety does not separate (i) from (iii). Both build for every size: (i) leaves the internal root P/24 wide (0.042 P), and P/16 leaves 0.053 P. A larger fraction leaves more room for a print's rounded crests and filled roots, a smaller one more engagement, and no measurement says which matters more until gate 20's prints. The rule takes (i) by the industry standard: it is the only fraction with a standard as its source, ISO 965-1's design profile of an external thread.
3. *What truncates.* (a) Crests cut back and roots cut deeper; (b) crests cut back and roots filled; (c) crests only. The rule takes (a), the safest: (b) crosses the class's maximum-material limit and meets a standard mate. (c) keeps the narrow roots where FDM's lines fill a groove's bottom. (a) gives each root the room (c) lacks at no cost in engagement.
4. *The clearance's direction.* (a) Radial, on every diameter (the profile moved as a whole); (b) normal to the flanks. The rule takes (a), as the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 set it (section 3, choice "How printer.fit reaches threads and holes"): it is the clearance of print.md's fit classes, a gap on the radius, and the spike's.
5. *Thread flanks in the overhang check.* (a) Report them as every overhang; (b) leave listed thread flanks out of the overhang findings up to `THREAD_FLANK_ANGLE`; (c) report them as a finding of their own at info severity; (d) a rule on every narrow overhang. The rule takes (b), the safest that tells the truth: a thread printed upright needs no support, and (a) tells every user to add supports inside a thread. (c) adds a `FindingCode` value, a protocol schema change, for a finding with nothing to do. (d) changes M1's results and expected files. Printed 60-degree threads are made upright without supports, the industry practice.

## Consequences

- features/thread.md section 5 and tests 13 to 18 specify the profile. Spike 8.9 measures section 8.2's profile (kernel.md section 8.9). Track 3A checks its Results again against this profile in stage 3 (the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19, section 2 item 5).
- The truncation and the clearance take engagement: features/thread.md open issue 1 records it, and gate 20's prints judge the start values.
- print.md's `AnalysisOptions` gains `thread_flanks` and `THREAD_FLANK_ANGLE` joins section 8's list for review. Test 34's serialization of the default options does not change.
- Track 3A fills `thread_flanks` in engine.md section 3.12 step 5 from the thread's and the threaded hole's flank roles. A thread modeled by hand or imported from STEP keeps its flank findings (print.md open issue 12).
