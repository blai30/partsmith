# W2-2C-2: Python names of the new curves, and how a spline names its points

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the M2 plan's open choice "Python names for bspline, fit_spline and fixed_spline, and how their points are given", decided as the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 accept it (section 3). It departs from the plan's recommendation in two points: the points of `through` and `control` are `Point` entities only, not `Point` or `PointRef` (decision 2, alternative 2), and `degree` defaults to the tool's `min(3, len(poles) - 1)`, not to 3 (decision 3, alternative 3). Needs human review at the M2 gate: it adds public Python names and arguments ([ADR 0048](0048-python-keywords-gui-round-trip-and-python-budgets.md), gap list PY-2) and the names they add to `RESERVED_VARIABLES` (m2-plan.md gate 19, the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19, section 2 item 12). It changes no file format, protocol, naming, license or dependency. It settles python-api.md section 1's question of one `Spline` or three, and the Python name of `fixed_spline` that features/sketch.md test 60's twin needs.

## Context

- **The records** (sketch.md section 2.12). Item 14 lands eight curve kinds: `ellipse`, `elliptical_arc`, `parabola`, `hyperbola`, `conic`, and three B-spline kinds. A `bspline` names its control points, and a `fit_spline` the points it runs through, as point entities of its sketch, by id. A `fixed_spline` holds its control points as coordinates. Every id names a point entity other than the spline (sketch.md section 3.20.5). A line's end, a role such as `line.end`, cannot be a pole.
- **Python mirrors records** (python-api.md sections 1 and 2.6). `Line` and `Arc` take their record's fields. Each constructor makes one record, and the round trip of section 3.9 holds byte for byte. Raw lengths and angles convert by the document units (section 3.2). No rule covers unitless numbers: a hyperbola's parameters, `rho`, knots and weights.
- **The tool's defaults** ([ADR 0078](0078-fit-spline-interpolation-and-spline-tool-defaults.md) decision 3). The control point spline tool writes degree `min(3, poles - 1)`, clamped uniform knots on the integers, and no weights.
- **Item 24** (wave 3) moves `Rectangle` and `Polygon` onto the core's recipes. A Python spline that made its own construction points from tuples would add a second place that builds what a recipe builds.
- **Other APIs.** build123d has `Spline(*points, tangents=...)` through points and `Bezier(*points, weights=...)` on control points. CadQuery's `spline` interpolates. FreeCAD's `Part.BSplineCurve` takes poles, multiplicities, knots and weights, and `interpolate` takes points.
- **Reserved names** (python-api.md section 3.8 rule 7). Generated variable names are lowercase, and a name in `partsmith.__all__` gets a suffix. The engine's `RESERVED_VARIABLES` (`crates/partsmith-engine/src/python.rs`) mirrors `__all__`.

## Decision

1. **One `Spline` with three constructors.** `Spline.through(points, *, periodic=False)` makes a `fit_spline`, `Spline.control(poles, *, degree=None, knots=None, weights=None, periodic=False)` a `bspline`, and `Spline.fixed(poles, *, degree=None, knots=None, weights=None, periodic=False)` a `fixed_spline`, each with `construction=` and `id=`. `Spline(...)` itself raises `TypeError`. Code generation writes each record with its constructor.
2. **Points are `Point` entities.** `through` and `control` take `Point` objects made in the same sketch block, in order, and store their ids. Python makes no point of its own. Generated code writes the `Point` lines first, as stored, and passes their variables. `fixed` takes coordinates, as its record holds them. This departs from the recommendation, which named `Point` or `PointRef` arguments: a `PointRef` names a role, and the record takes only point entities.
3. **Defaults follow the tool.** `degree=None` is `min(3, len(poles) - 1)`, `knots=None` the tool's knots for that degree, pole count and `periodic`, and `weights=None` no weights. Code generation writes `degree=`, `knots=` and `weights=` only where the record differs. A tool's spline reads `Spline.control([point_1, point_2, point_3, point_4], id="e-5")`.
4. **The other kinds mirror their records.** `Ellipse(center, major, minor_radius)`, `EllipticalArc(center, major, minor_radius, start_angle, end_angle)`, `Parabola(vertex, focus, start_parameter, end_parameter)`, `Hyperbola(center, major, minor_radius, start_parameter, end_parameter)` and `Conic(start, control, end, rho)`. `major` is the record's vector from the center to the end of the major semi-axis. Each object has its kind's roles as `PointRef` attributes (`.center`, `.major`, `.minor`, `.vertex`, `.focus`, `.control`, `.start`, `.end`).
5. **Unitless numbers stay as given.** A hyperbola's parameters, `rho`, knots and weights take `int` or `float` only and are stored as given. Lengths (a parabola's parameters among them) and angles convert as section 3.2's raw numbers do.
6. **Names.** `partsmith.__all__` gains `Ellipse`, `EllipticalArc`, `Parabola`, `Hyperbola`, `Conic` and `Spline`, after `Arc`, and `RESERVED_VARIABLES` gains them too. No generated variable changes: rule 7's names are lowercase.

## Alternatives considered

Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the most open-source approach, then the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Every option is in-house code under Apache-2.0. The first criterion ties.

1. *Names.* (i) One `Spline` with keywords (`points=` for a fit spline, `poles=` for the others); (ii) three classes `FitSpline`, `BSpline` and `FixedSpline`; (iii) one `Spline` with a constructor per record. (i) leaves the record a call makes to the keywords it got, and a call with both `points=` and `poles=` needs a rule. (ii) adds three public names for one curve. (iii) maps each call to one record and adds one name. All three round-trip. The rule takes (iii), the safest: each constructor has one meaning and one check, and `Spline` is the name python-api.md section 1 and m2-plan.md item 33 use.
2. *Points.* (i) `Point` entities only; (ii) tuples too, which make construction points before the spline; (iii) wait for item 24's recipes in Python. (ii) builds in Python what a recipe builds in the core, and its order of hidden entities becomes a contract of the round trip. (iii) leaves splines out of Python in wave 2, while gate 2 wants twins for item 14's examples. The rule takes (i), the safest. The recommendation's `PointRef` falls away, as Context says.
3. *Defaults of `degree` and `knots`.* (i) Required arguments; (ii) the tool's defaults, written only when they differ; (iii) a fixed `degree=3`. (iii) fails for two or three poles, where the tool writes a lower degree. (i) makes every generated call carry its knots. The rule takes (ii): the same records, and a script that reads like the tool's output.
4. *Unitless numbers.* (i) Numbers only; (ii) numbers, or an `Expr` or a `str` the engine evaluates as a scalar, as section 3.2 evaluates other raw numbers. The record stores a number either way, and nothing keeps it parametric. The rule takes (i), the safest: an expression would look parametric and would not be, and a unit on `rho` or a knot has no meaning.
