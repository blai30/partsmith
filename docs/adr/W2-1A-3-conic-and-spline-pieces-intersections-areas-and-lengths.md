# W2-1A-3: Conic and spline pieces, intersections, areas and lengths in the sketch crate

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): how `build_profile`, the region union and chains handle the curve kinds of the M2 plan's item 14 (wave 2), the plan's open choices "Intersection method of the arrangement for new kinds", "A spline that crosses itself" and "How a piece of a split conic or spline is represented", as the wave 2 decisions of 2026-11-19 (section 2 item 2) fix the last one. It changes neither the file format nor the protocol schema: `ProfileCurve` is not stored and not in `partsmith-protocol`. One part goes to human review at the M2 gate (m2-plan.md gate 19): the arc-length method of decision 5, because its result is the `l=` text of a `ChainFingerprint`, which documents store from the sweep on and which freezes with format version 3 ([ADR 0061](0061-format-version-3-for-the-records-of-m2.md)).

## Context

- **M1's arrangement.** sketch.md section 3.13.2 intersects lines, circles and arcs in closed form. Vertices merge within `PROFILE_TOLERANCE` (1e-6 mm), and every edge's ends are vertex coordinates, so consecutive edges of a loop share their ends bitwise. Areas and centroids are exact (section 3.13.4). Chain lengths are exact (section 3.18.2).
- **The new kinds.** [ADR 0066](0066-sketch-entities-of-format-version-3-operations-and-display-polylines.md) adds ellipses, elliptical arcs, parabolas, hyperbolas, rho conics and three B-spline kinds. sketch.md section 3.20.10 gives `ProfileCurve` the variants `Ellipse`, `EllipticalArc`, `Parabola`, `Hyperbola` and `Bspline`, the last also for conics and fit splines, and leaves their fields, intersections, areas and chains to item 14's spec.
- **The kernel.** kernel.md section 2.5 builds each variant as an exact OCCT curve. A `Bspline` is a `Geom2d_BSplineCurve`. The wave 2 decisions (section 2 item 2) give it an optional parameter range, built as a `Geom2d_TrimmedCurve` over the curve and checked against its neighbors within `LINEAR`. Track 1B writes that in kernel.md.
- **Risks.** m2-plan.md risk 5 asks for a deterministic arrangement within 1e-9 across x86_64 and wasm32. Conic-conic intersections are roots of a quartic, which is ill-conditioned at a tangency. M1 curves never cross themselves. A B-spline can.
- **Numbers that reach documents.** A region's fingerprint (`c=..|a=..`, 6 decimals) and a chain's fingerprint (`s=..|e=..|l=..`) are stored in references. Neither chooses geometry: a region matches by key and boundary first, a chain by key only (sketch.md sections 3.14 and 3.18.3).

## Decision

1. **Spans.** Every curve of a new kind, and every line, circle and arc that meets one, is turned into Bezier spans for the arrangement: lines as one span of degree 1, circles and arcs and ellipses and elliptical arcs as rational quadratic spans of at most a quarter turn, a parabola as one quadratic span, a hyperbola as rational quadratic spans of at most 1 in its parameter, a conic as its one rational quadratic span, and a B-spline by knot insertion at every interior knot (sketch.md section 3.13.2). M1's closed forms stay for pairs of lines, circles and arcs, so no M1 result changes.
2. **Intersections by subdivision.** Two curves of which at least one is new are intersected span by span: their control polygons' boxes are tested and the larger span is halved until both are small, then each candidate is polished by Newton's method on the exact curves, with a closest-approach step at a tangency. Iteration counts, depths and order are fixed, so the result is deterministic within a build.
3. **A spline that crosses itself** is split at its own crossings. The finding is the existing `self_intersection` with the entity twice, `entities: [a, a]`. Regions still form.
4. **A piece of a conic or spline** is the whole curve plus a parameter range. `ProfileCurve::Bspline` holds the B-spline in the curve's own parameterization, `range` (`None` for the whole curve), the piece's `start` and `end` as arrangement vertices, shared bitwise with its neighbors, and `forward`, its direction along the parameter. Pieces of ellipses, parabolas and hyperbolas hold their end vertices, as an `Arc` does. The region union merges consecutive pieces of one entity into one curve, so one entity still gives one lateral face.
5. **Areas, centroids and lengths.** Areas and centroids are exact for lines, arcs, ellipses, elliptical arcs, parabolas, hyperbolas and polynomial B-splines (closed forms, and Gauss-Legendre rules exact for the polynomial integrands). Rational spans (conics and weighted B-splines) and every length of a new kind are integrated by 16-point Gauss-Legendre on each span, halving the subintervals until two results agree within 1e-13 relative, at most 2^12 subintervals per span. The length of a chain and its `l=` text use this rule.

## Alternatives considered

Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the most open-source approach, then the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Every option below is in-house Rust under Apache-2.0 with no new dependency (m2-plan.md choice 10), so the first criterion ties throughout.

1. *The intersection method.* (i) Closed forms for line-conic and conic-conic pairs (quadratics and quartics), subdivision for splines; (ii) every new kind as rational Bezier spans, with subdivision and Newton polishing; (iii) intersect the display polylines, then refine on the exact curves. The rule takes (ii), the safest. One path serves every pair, with fixed iteration counts, so its results agree across targets to rounding (risks 5 and 7). A quartic's roots move by the square root of its rounding at a tangency, which (i) would meet at every tangent touch. (iii) ties the arrangement to display data, which section 3.20.9 keeps out of every feature. Subdivision with Newton polishing is the textbook method (Sederberg and Nishita's Bezier clipping family; Piegl and Tiller, "The NURBS Book", 1997).
2. *A spline that crosses itself.* (i) Split it at its own crossings and report `self_intersection` with the entity twice; (ii) a new finding that drops the spline from profiles; (iii) no support. The rule takes (i), the safest. No `ProfileFinding` variant is added, so the protocol schema is unchanged. Regions form as they do for two lines that cross, which Fusion does too. (ii) loses every region the spline bounds. (iii) leaves the arrangement undefined.
3. *How a piece is held.* (i) The whole curve plus a parameter range, trimmed in the kernel; (ii) a sub-B-spline cut by in-house knot insertion; (iii) the end points only, projected to parameters by the kernel. The rule takes (i), the safest, which the wave 2 decisions fix (section 2 item 2). It is exact. It is unambiguous on closed and self-crossing curves, where (iii) is not. It makes the union's merge a union of ranges. (ii) changes the poles of every piece, so a merged loop would no longer be the user's curve bitwise.
4. *Areas of rational spans.* (i) Adaptive Gauss-Legendre with a stated stop rule; (ii) closed forms for each conic type through its center and axes; (iii) polygon approximation. The rule takes (i), the safest. A rational quadratic span becomes an arc of an ellipse, parabola or hyperbola, and (ii) would need three formulas and the conversion near the parabola, where both others break down. (iii) is not exact enough for the 6-decimal fingerprint. Weighted B-splines of higher degree have no closed form at all.
5. *The arc-length method.* (i) Gauss-Legendre on spans with halving until two results agree; (ii) a fixed number of points per span; (iii) the length of the display polyline. The rule takes (i), the safest. The length of a curve of a new kind has no closed form (an elliptic integral for an ellipse). (ii) has no stated accuracy for a thin ellipse or a spline with a sharp bend. (iii) is off by up to 0.01 mm per segment and is display data. The stop rule makes the result agree with the exact length within 1e-9 relative, so the 6-decimal `l=` text is exact except where rounding meets a tie, as for region fingerprints. This needs human review at the gate (decision 5).
6. *Overlaps of new kinds.* (i) Two curves overlap when they lie on the same curve within the tolerance (same kind and defining data), plus a fallback: a pair of spans that subdivision cannot separate is an overlap from its first to its last candidate; (ii) a general test of any two curves that run together along a stretch. The rule takes (i), the recommendation. (ii) needs a distance function between curves of different kinds, such as a conic drawn over an arc of an ellipse, which fixed steps cannot decide. The fallback reports such a stretch as an overlap, with ends that depend on where subdivision stopped. sketch.md open issue 29 records it.

## Consequences

- Specs: sketch.md sections 2.7 (the variants of `ProfileCurve`), 3.13.1 to 3.13.4, 3.14, 3.18, 3.19 and 4.3, tests 342 and on, open issue 29. kernel.md section 2.5 mirrors decision 4 in `PlanarCurve` (track 1B). features.md section 3.7 converts each variant (track 1C).
- No M1 result changes: pairs of lines, circles and arcs keep their closed forms, and no test of M1 or wave 1 changes.
- The arrangement gets slower with splines. sketch.md section 5.3 gains spline rows, measured by ADR 0057's rule.
- Gate 19 reviews the arc-length method (decision 5).
