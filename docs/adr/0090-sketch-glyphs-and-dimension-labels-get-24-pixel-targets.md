# 0090: Sketch glyphs and dimension labels get 24-pixel targets

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05): the M2 plan's item 16 and section 7.3 item 22, option (b), with the offsets of the choices below. It amends [ADR 0049](0049-web-content-security-policy-and-accessibility-checks.md) decision 3, a decision the owner made for M1 (option (a), the marks keep their sizes). It goes to human review at the M2 gate (m2-plan.md gate 19, which names this ADR). No file format, protocol, naming, license, dependency or desktop capability change. It resolves ui.md open issue 42 for the glyphs and the labels. The rollback marker, the view cube's cells and the repair candidate markers keep ADR 0049's exceptions.

## Context

- **The marks of M1** (read from the tree at 0f578c1). A constraint glyph is a `size-4.5` button (18 CSS pixels at UI scale 1). Its center sits 14 pixels off its curve on screen, on the side facing up the screen (`GLYPH_OFFSET_PIXELS` and `stackedCenters` in `ui/src/sketch/screen.ts`). Further glyphs on the same anchor stack 20 pixels apart along the curve (`GLYPH_STACK_PIXELS`). A glyph at a point sits 14 pixels from it along the diagonal up and to the right, and further ones stack 20 pixels to the right. A line's glyphs sit at its middle. A circle's sit at its point at 45 degrees, and an arc's there when the arc reaches it, else at its middle (`curveAnchor` in `ui/src/sketch/annotations.ts`). A dimension label is a `text-xs` chip 18 pixels high (ui.md 3.16.2). The used marks of ui.md 3.16.10 (`size-4`, `role="img"`, not pickable) follow the glyphs on their anchor, one stack step after the last.
- **What WCAG 2.2 asks.** Success criterion 2.5.8 (AA) asks 24 by 24 CSS pixels for a pointer target, or a smaller target whose 24-pixel circle meets no other target. Two stacked glyphs 20 pixels apart fail both. ui.md 1.1 claims AA, and 3.24 states the rule. ADR 0049 left the glyphs, the labels, the rollback marker, the view cube's cells and the repair candidate markers out of E-163.
- **The owner's word.** ADR 0049's status: the marks keep their sizes for M1, and this is revisited after M1. m2-plan.md item 16 and section 7.3 item 22 take option (b) for glyphs and labels by a new ADR before the overlay is laid out again. M2 adds about a dozen constraint glyphs (item 15).
- **Where the pointer picks in sketch mode.** `hitTest` in `ui/src/sketch/hit.ts` picks a curve within 7.5 CSS pixels of its center line (`CURVE_HIT_PIXELS`, half the 7-pixel pick width of ui.md 3.6 plus its 4-pixel radius) and a point within 9.5 pixels (`POINT_HIT_PIXELS`, half the 11-pixel pick square plus the radius), times the UI scale. The overlay's buttons sit above the canvas and take every click inside them. A 24-pixel button centered 14 pixels off a horizontal curve would have its near edge 2 pixels from the line, inside that 7.5-pixel band.
- **How far a square reaches.** The buttons are squares aligned with the screen. Centered off a curve whose unit tangent on screen is `t`, a 24-pixel square reaches `12 × (|t.x| + |t.y|)` pixels toward the curve: 12 for a horizontal or vertical curve, 16.97 at 45 degrees. Two such squares with centers `d` apart along `t` overlap unless `d × max(|t.x|, |t.y|)` is at least 24. A fixed offset of 20 pixels and a fixed step of 24 hold only for horizontal and vertical curves. At 45 degrees, where every circle's glyphs sit, the square would come within 3.03 pixels of the curve, and two stacked squares would overlap by about 7 pixels in each axis.
- **Arcs.** On an arc whose middle is in its lower half on screen, the side facing up the screen is the inside, toward the center. The arc then bends toward the glyph. A button 20 pixels off such an arc covers its pick band when the arc's radius on screen is under about 152 pixels.

## Decision

1. **Glyph buttons.** Each constraint glyph is a `size-6` button (24 CSS pixels, times the UI scale through rem) that holds the unchanged `size-4.5` chip, centered (ui.md 3.16.2). The button has no fill and no border of its own. The chip keeps its fill, border, icon and states. Hover, focus ring, click and selection belong to the button.
2. **Label buttons.** Each dimension label is a button of at least `min-h-6 min-w-6` that holds the unchanged `text-xs` chip, centered. A one-character label (`5`) is 24 pixels wide too.
3. **Offsets.** With `t` the curve's unit tangent on screen at the anchor and every length times the UI scale, a glyph on a curve is centered `8 + 12 × (|t.x| + |t.y|)` pixels off the curve: 20 for a horizontal or vertical curve, about 24.97 at 45 degrees (`GLYPH_CLEARANCE_PIXELS` 8 and `GLYPH_TARGET_PIXELS` 24 in `screen.ts`, in place of M1's `GLYPH_OFFSET_PIXELS`). The button's corner nearest the curve's tangent line at the anchor is then 8 pixels from that line, outside the 7.5 pixels within which the pointer picks the curve. On a line the glyph stays on the side facing up the screen (right for a vertical line). On a circle or an arc it is on the side away from its center's projection. The projection of a circle or an arc lies on the center's side of every tangent line and bends away from the button. The button then keeps at least 8 pixels from it. A glyph at a point is centered 30 pixels from it along the diagonal up and to the right (`POINT_GLYPH_OFFSET_PIXELS` 30, about 21.21 pixels right and 21.21 up). Its corner nearest the point is then about 13.03 pixels from the point, outside the 9.5-pixel pick.
4. **Stacking.** Further glyphs on the same curve anchor stack `24 / max(|t.x|, |t.y|)` pixels apart along the curve: 24 for a horizontal or vertical curve, about 33.94 at 45 degrees. Further glyphs at a point stack 24 pixels apart to the right. Neighboring buttons of one anchor then touch along one screen axis and never overlap, and a stacked button on a line or outside a circle keeps the first one's clearance. A used mark follows the last glyph one stack step later, as in M1. A used mark at a point sits where a glyph there would.
5. **Glyphs of different anchors.** They may overlap where their anchors are closer on screen than a button, as in M1. Zooming in moves them apart, since anchors are in millimeters and offsets in pixels. Laying them out around each other waits for a measured need (ui.md open issue 42).
6. **Tests.** The numbers of `screen.test.ts` and `session.test.ts` change by spec first (ui.md U-298). E-163 stops leaving out `constraint-glyph` and `sketch-dimension` and runs a sixteenth state on a new fixture (`glyph-stack.ptsm`, ui.md 6.2): glyphs stacked on a horizontal line, at a circle's 45-degree point and on a 45-degree line, a point glyph and a label. It also checks that no two glyph buttons overlap. U-300 and E-187 check the clearances and steps on that fixture. ui.md 3.24 lists only the rollback marker, the view cube's cells and the repair candidate markers as marks.

## Alternatives considered

For the decision (ADR 0049's labels for decision 3):

- (a) **Keep M1's marks.** No layout change. The glyphs and labels stay under WCAG 2.2's minimum, and stacked glyphs fail its spacing exception. The owner's M1 choice, revisited by m2-plan.md section 7.3 item 22.
- (b) **24-pixel buttons around the unchanged chips** (chosen).
- (c) **A `w-6` rollback marker** around its unchanged line. It widens every gap of the timeline strip by 16 pixels. The marker keeps WCAG's equivalent exception ("Roll back to here" in each item's menu and the marker's keys).
- (d) **Axe** as a development dependency (MPL-2.0). A new dependency for review that checks less of the tab order than E-162.

The rule takes (b). (a) to (c) add no code from outside the project, and (d) adds an open-source dependency. Open source does not decide. (b) is the safest: it lays the overlay out once, before item 15's glyphs arrive, and E-163 then measures every glyph and label. The industry standard is WCAG 2.2 AA, the level ui.md already claims, which (b) meets. The plan recommends (b), and (c) is not needed for the marker's equivalent exception.

For the offset and the step (plan choice "Glyph offset and label width"):

- (i) **Keep 14 pixels and 20.** A 24-pixel button's near edge is 2 pixels from a horizontal line, inside its pick band. A click on a line under a glyph selects the glyph.
- (ii) **A fixed 20 pixels on a curve, 30 at a point, a 24-pixel step.** The plan's numbers, held fixed. It clears the pick band and keeps buttons apart only on horizontal and vertical curves.
- (iii) **An offset and a step that follow the curve's direction on screen, 30 pixels at a point, arcs on their outer side** (chosen).
- (iv) **A fixed 20 pixels, stacked along the screen's x axis** as M1 stacks at points. A step along x moves a stacked button toward a diagonal line on one side of it.

The rule takes (iii). All four are the project's own code. (iii) is the safest: it is the one whose buttons clear the pick band and stay apart on every curve and in every view. On horizontal and vertical curves, where (ii)'s fixed numbers work, it gives the same 20 and 24 pixels. Constraint icons beside their curve, not on it, are what Fusion and FreeCAD show, and (ii) to (iv) all do that. The plan's recommendation, at least 20 pixels (12 + 7.5) and a point offset that clears the point, holds in (iii). At a point, 27 pixels is the smallest whole number that clears the 9.5-pixel pick by the curves' half-pixel margin (its corner 0.53 pixels beyond it). 30 puts the corner 3.53 pixels beyond it, on M1's diagonal.

For the label width: **`min-h-6` only** leaves a one-digit label 18 pixels wide, which fails WCAG 2.2's 24-pixel rule. **`min-h-6 min-w-6`** (chosen) makes every label meet it, whatever its text. The rule takes the second. Both are the project's own classes. The second is the safer: E-163 can then measure every label against one number, where the first fails it for a short label such as `5`. The industry standard is WCAG 2.2 AA, which only the second meets. The plan recommends `min-h-6 min-w-6`.

## Consequences

- The overlay's layout changes for every sketch. Glyphs move 6 pixels further from horizontal and vertical curves and about 11 further at 45 degrees. A stack step grows by 4 pixels on horizontal and vertical curves and by about 14 at 45 degrees. Point glyphs move 16 pixels further out along the diagonal. An arc whose middle is in its lower half gets its glyphs outside. The visual baselines that show glyphs or labels (ui.md 6.6: V-5 and V-17) are taken again in the change that builds this ADR. V-26 and V-27 are new, and the owner looks at all four at the gate.
- Every test that reads a glyph's position or a stack step changes by spec first: `screen.test.ts` (its used mark at a point among them), `session.test.ts` (the glyph 14 pixels above its line) and U-298. E-tests that click a glyph find it by its test id, not by position, and do not change.
- A glyph can cover another curve that passes near its anchor. That curve stays pickable outside the button. The M1 overlay had the same property at 14 pixels.
- Reverting is the constants, the offset and step rule of `stackedCenters`, two class lists, the E-163 exclusions and the visual baselines.
