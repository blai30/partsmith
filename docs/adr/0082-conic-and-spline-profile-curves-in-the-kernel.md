# 0082: Conic and spline profile curves in the kernel

Status: Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05); needs human review at the M2 gate for its change to the kernel facade's public API, the rule of [ADR 0026](0026-kernel-facade-additions-for-errors.md): `PlanarCurve` gains five variants and the value type `Vector2`, and `PlanarCurve` and `TaggedCurve` lose `Copy`, a breaking change for `partsmith-features` that builds them. It specifies the kernel half of the M2 plan's item 14 in kernel.md sections 2.5, 3.4.2, 3.4.4 and 3.6: the `PlanarCurve` variants of the sketch's conics and splines, how a piece of a split spline reaches the kernel, how the kernel checks and measures them, and the revolve's half-plane check on them. It changes no file format, protocol schema, naming algorithm, license or dependency, and needs no new OCCT toolkit: `Geom2d` is in `TKG2d`, `GeomLib`, `Extrema`, `GCPnts` and `Geom2dConvert` are in `TKGeomBase`, and `BRepGProp` is in `TKTopAlgo`, all already linked (kernel.md section 3.12 item 3).

## Context

- **What the plan fixed.** m2-plan.md choice 11 builds the sketch's curves as exact OCCT curves, with conics and fit splines as rational or polynomial B-splines. kernel.md section 2.5 (wave 1) named the variants `Ellipse`, `EllipticalArc`, `Parabola`, `Hyperbola` and `Bspline`, one per `ProfileCurve` variant of sketch.md section 3.20.10, with the same names. It left their fields, checks, messages, signed areas and tests to item 14's kernel spec.
- **Pieces.** The sketch's arrangement splits curves at their intersections (sketch.md section 3.13.2). A piece of an arc keeps its circle and takes the vertices as its ends. A piece of a B-spline cannot be described by its end points alone: on a closed or self-crossing spline a point can lie at several parameters. kernel.md section 2.5 as written had no field for a piece. The decisions of 2026-11-19 (wave 2's spec round, item 2) take option (a) of the plan's choice "How a piece of a split conic or spline is represented": the whole curve plus a parameter range, built in the shim as a `Geom2d_TrimmedCurve` and checked against its neighbors within `LINEAR`. Track 1A writes the matching `ProfileCurve` fields in sketch.md section 2.7 and the ADR of the sketch side.
- **What OCCT 7.9.3 requires** (read in its sources on 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-23). `gp_Elips2d` refuses a minor radius larger than the major radius. sketch.md section 3.20.1 allows one. `gp_Hypr2d` takes any two radii. `Geom2d_BSplineCurve` refuses a degree above 25 (`BSplCLib::MaxDegree()`), fewer than 2 poles, a knot gap of at most `Epsilon` of the knot before it and a weight of at most `gp::Resolution()` (`DBL_MIN`). `Epsilon` (`Standard_Real.hxx`) is the distance to the next larger double, except at 0, where `NextAfter` returns `DBL_MIN`. `BSplCLib::NbPoles` counts poles for end multiplicities from 1 to `degree + 1`, and an open spline whose end multiplicity is below `degree + 1` has a domain that starts after its first knot (`FirstParameter` is `flatknots(degree + 1)`). The periodic form takes `poles + 1` knots of multiplicity 1, and its span from knot `j` combines the `degree + 1` poles from pole `j` on, cyclically. `BRepLib::BuildCurve3d` of an edge on a `Geom_Plane` is `GeomLib::To3d`, exact for lines, conics, B-splines and trimmed curves. `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeEdge2d` builds on `BRepLib::Plane()`, a `Geom_Plane` that OCCT creates on its first call and keeps for the process.
- **What OCCT measures** (probes against OCCT 7.9.3 on 2026-11-23). `BRepGProp`'s default integrations put a planar face bounded by a B-spline of several spans up to 1.4e-2 off its area and its extrusion up to 9.6e-3 off its volume. `BRepGProp_Vinert` with a precision of 1e-12 misreads the `Extrusion` face of a closed spline's extrusion (2.2e-3 off). `BRepGProp::SurfaceProperties` with a precision of 1e-12 on planar faces and Gauss-Kronrod (`BRepGProp_VinertGK`) on volumes measured every case within 1e-13. `Geom2d_BSplineCurve` builds knots 1e-10 apart, and `GCPnts_AbscissaPoint::Length` then measures half the curve.
- **What the facade does today.** `make_profile` checks, chains and orients every loop in Rust (`src/profiles.rs`): it takes the ends of lines and arcs from their fields and sums the signed area of lines, arcs and circles in closed form. The revolve's half-plane check (`src/sweeps.rs`) takes exact extremes of lines, arcs and circles. This crate depends only on `partsmith-mesh`. It cannot call the sketch's in-house spline evaluation (m2-plan.md choice 10).

## Decision

1. **Variants with the sketch's geometry.** `PlanarCurve` gains `Ellipse { center, major, minor_radius }`, `EllipticalArc { center, major, minor_radius, start, end }`, `Parabola { vertex, focus, start, end }`, `Hyperbola { center, major, minor_radius, start, end }` and `Bspline { degree, poles, weights, knots, multiplicities, periodic, range }`, with the value type `Vector2` for `major`. They carry the geometry of sketch.md section 2.7's `ProfileCurve` variants without the direction flags (`ccw`, `forward`), as `Arc` does. A `Bspline` leaves out the sketch's `start` and `end` (the kernel measures them) and takes its knots in OCCT's form: the sketch's runs of equal knot values become distinct knots with multiplicities (features.md section 3.7 converts them by kernel.md section 2.5 item 1's rule). `PlanarCurve` and `TaggedCurve` derive `Clone` and no longer `Copy`.
2. **A piece is the whole curve plus its range.** A piece of a spline that the sketch split is the whole `Bspline` with `range`, its parameter interval `[low, high]` in the spline's own parameter (on a periodic spline it may run past the last knot). The shim builds it as a `Geom2d_TrimmedCurve` and measures its points at `low` and `high`, and the chain checks them against the neighbors within `LINEAR`. A piece of an ellipse, a parabola or a hyperbola is an `EllipticalArc`, `Parabola` or `Hyperbola` with the piece's ends. The facade checks those ends against the conic, and the shim trims the conic at the nearest points of its junction vertices, as it trims an arc. These points are unique, and the conics need no range.
3. **Exact curves on the profile's own plane.** The shim builds each curve as an exact `Geom2d` curve in plane coordinates and its edge with `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeEdge` on the profile's `Geom_Plane`, with `BRepLib::BuildCurve3d` for the 3D curve. It never uses `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeEdge2d` or `BRepLib::Plane()`. An ellipse whose minor radius is the larger is built with its axes swapped: the same points in the same direction. A hyperbola never is.
4. **Knots in OCCT's form, with the kernel's own limits.** `Bspline` carries distinct knots and multiplicities. The kernel checks OCCT's rules (`MaxDegree`, `Epsilon` with its value at 0, `BSplCLib::NbPoles`) and three of its own. An open spline is clamped: its first and last multiplicities are `degree + 1`, as the sketch makes every open spline. A periodic spline's knots give at least `degree + 1` poles. Each knot lies at least 1e-6 of the knot range beyond the one before it. The sketch's knot vector maps to the kernel's form by runs of equal values. A periodic spline's `poles + 1` values become knots of multiplicity 1 in OCCT's periodic convention, and the sketch's evaluation shares that convention (kernel.md section 2.5 items 1 and 5, open issue 24).
5. **The shim measures, the facade decides.** Once every curve of the call has passed the structure checks (sizes, splines and ranges), and before any loop is checked, the facade gives every `Bspline` to the shim's `measure_splines`. That call returns the spline's points at the ends of its range, its length and its signed area term. The revolve gives each spline to `spline_extremes`. Every check, message and orientation stays in Rust, in kernel.md section 3.4.2's order. Conics are measured in Rust in closed form, with their nearest points found by Newton's method.
6. **Checks with the facade's own messages.** Sizes of conics, the spline structure, the range, closed curves alone in their loop, zero length and a conic arc's ends on its conic, each with its `InvalidInput` sentence of kernel.md section 4.2. sketch.md section 3.20.5 states the same spline rules as OCCT and decision 4 since 2026-11-23 (sketch.md test 319), and the two rule sets must stay equal (kernel.md open issue 24).
7. **Exact extremes for the revolve.** The half-plane check takes each conic's extremes in closed form and each spline's within 1e-9 mm by Bezier segments, the convex hull of their poles and subdivision. The poles alone never decide.
8. **Precise measures of the faces and solids they make.** A planar face with a boundary curve other than a line or a circular arc is measured by `BRepGProp::SurfaceProperties` at relative precision 1e-12 (kernel.md section 3.6). In a volume, such a face and every face on an `Extrusion` or `Revolution` surface are integrated by Gauss-Kronrod (`BRepGProp_VinertGK`) at 1e-12, and a solid that holds one takes its center of mass from `BRepGProp::VolumePropertiesGK`. The area of an `Extrusion` or `Revolution` face and the length of a conic or spline edge keep OCCT's defaults and miss 1e-6 until track 2B specifies their fix (kernel.md open issue 22).

## Alternatives considered

Every option is open source, built on OCCT and on Partsmith's own code. The first criterion of the rule picks none of them. The rule then takes the safest, then the industry standard, then this work's recommendation. Each choice is Accepted (owner's rule of 2026-11-05).

1. *A piece of a spline or conic* (decided by the decisions of 2026-11-19, item 2). The conic of that item is the sketch's `conic` entity, a rational quadratic B-spline (sketch.md section 3.20.1): this choice covers every curve that reaches the kernel as a `Bspline`, the sketch's `bspline`, `fixed_spline`, `conic` and `fit_spline`. Options: (a) the whole curve plus a parameter range, trimmed in the kernel; (b) the sketch inserts knots until the piece is a B-spline of its own, whose ends are its vertices; (c) the end points only, the kernel projecting them to parameters. (a) is the safest. It is exact and unambiguous on closed and self-crossing curves, and the sketch's region union can merge pieces as unions of ranges. (b) moves the sketch's knot insertion into the kernel's input and rounds the piece's poles. (c) is ambiguous where a point lies at several parameters. A trimmed basis curve is also how OCCT itself describes a piece (`Geom2d_TrimmedCurve`), the industry standard.
2. *Where splines are evaluated for the checks and the orientation.* Options: (i) the shim measures and the facade decides; (ii) a second spline evaluation in this crate's Rust; (iii) the shim checks and orients every loop. The rule takes (i), the safest: OCCT evaluates the curve the shim builds, and every message and its order stays in Rust, where M1 put them. (ii) keeps a second evaluator in step with the sketch's and OCCT's. (iii) moves M1's tested orientation code into C++ and spreads the checks over two languages.
3. *Conic arcs by points or by parameters.* This covers the true conics, the ellipse, the elliptical arc, the parabola and the hyperbola, which reach the kernel as exact curves and not as B-splines. Options: (i) `start` and `end` points, the parameters taken from the nearest points; (ii) a parameter range as for splines. The rule takes (i), the safest: the points are the sketch's shared vertices, and a conic's nearest point is unique within `LINEAR` of it. Alternative 1's reason against end points does not hold here: no point of an elliptical arc, a parabola or one branch of a hyperbola lies at two of its parameters. The decisions' item 2 is thereby kept for every curve whose points can be ambiguous, and sketch.md section 2.7 passes conic arcs by their points the same way. (ii) would carry the same information twice, and the two copies could disagree. (i) is also the form `Arc` already has.
4. *Extremes of a spline for the revolve.* Options: (i) exact within 1e-9 mm by subdivision; (ii) the convex hull of the poles. The rule takes (i), the safest: (ii) refuses profiles whose poles cross the axis while the curve does not. The kernel would then refuse what the features accept, and its hint would call the failure a bug.
5. *An ellipse whose minor radius is the larger.* Options: (i) swap its axes in the kernel; (ii) refuse it. The rule takes (i), the safest: the swapped ellipse is the same curve, and (ii) would refuse an ellipse that sketch.md section 3.20.1 accepts, with a hint that calls the failure a bug.
6. *Knots that lie very close.* Options: (i) refuse a gap below 1e-6 of the knot range; (ii) accept every gap that `Geom2d_BSplineCurve` accepts; (iii) refuse a gap below a fixed length of parameter. The rule takes (i), the safest: with (ii) a gap of 5e-11 of the range builds a curve whose length OCCT measures as half of it, and a gap of 1e-200 fails in `BRepLib` with `Failed`. (iii) ties the limit to the parameter's scale, and the sketch is free to choose that scale. A span of less than 1e-6 of the range carries no shape a user can see.
7. *An open spline that is not clamped.* Options: (i) refuse it; (ii) accept it and measure its ends at OCCT's `FirstParameter` and `LastParameter`. The rule takes (i), the safest: the sketch never makes one (sketch.md section 3.20.5), and (ii) would give every range check a domain that differs from the knots.
8. *How faces and solids of these curves are measured.* Options: (i) `BRepGProp::SurfaceProperties` with a precision of 1e-12 on planar faces and Gauss-Kronrod on volumes; (ii) the shim's exact Green path extended to conics and splines on planes, with Gauss-Kronrod for the swept faces; (iii) OCCT's defaults. (i) and (ii) are equally safe on the measured faces, within 1e-13. (iii) missed by up to 1.4e-2. The industry standard does not pick between (i) and (ii). This work recommends (i): it is less shim code (AGENTS.md principle 5).

## Consequences

- kernel.md sections 2.1, 2.5, 3.4.2 item 7, 3.4.4, 3.6 items 1 to 3, 3.10 item 9, 4.2, 5 and tests 191 to 210 state it. The bridge's `ProfileCurveData` gains fields and `make_profile` a slice of `SplineData` (kernel.md section 2.5 item 7).
- partsmith-features converts each new `ProfileCurve` variant to its `PlanarCurve` one to one (features.md section 3.7, track 1C), and passes a split spline's piece unchanged.
- sketch.md section 2.7's `ProfileCurve` (track 1A, 2026-11-19) carries this geometry: its `Bspline` has `range: Option<[f64; 2]>` and a sketch knot vector that features.md section 3.7 groups into knots and multiplicities. sketch.md sections 3.20.5 and 3.20.8 refuse the splines the kernel refuses (track 1A, 2026-11-23, sketch.md test 319): knots within `Epsilon(0)` = `DBL_MIN` of 0, gaps below 1e-6 of the knot range and subnormal weights. The two rule sets must stay equal (kernel.md open issue 24).
- The edges report `Ellipse`, `Parabola`, `Hyperbola` and `BsplineCurve` (kernel.md section 3.6 item 3), and their extruded and revolved faces `Extrusion` and `Revolution`. Naming's fingerprints already hold these kinds.
- Decision 8 also reaches M1's bodies: a planar face bounded by an ellipse or a B-spline, such as the face where an oblique plane cuts a cylinder, is now measured precisely. An example whose expected area or volume was recorded at the defaults may need it recorded again, closer to the exact value. Gauss-Kronrod took up to 18.2 ms for the volume of a small extrusion where the default took 0.26 ms (kernel.md section 5).
- The core size of the change is recorded (kernel.md section 5, [ADR 0076](0076-occt-built-for-the-web-at-oz.md) decision 6).
